## Recipe card

`RecipeDetailModal` (design: `docs/design/riso-v2-recipe-card/`; pieces in `RecipeCardParts.jsx`, logic in `lib/recipeCard.js` with tests) is **the one card for a recipe**. `App.jsx` renders it once and opens it with `openRecipeCard(recipeOrId, from?)` (`from` is the box of the card it grows out of), over the page you are on: the back pill says that page ("← Planner", `pageLabel`), and closing goes back to it. It replaced the recipe pop-out: every page opens this card in one tap.

- **Computer** (≥ 768px): two columns, `440px | 1fr` (under 1180px: `360px | 1fr`, three footer cards, and the timer pills under the step text), with "Uses the same ingredients" along the bottom. Left: the photo (340px, ← back pill, "1 / 3 photos", ← → flip, tap for the full-size viewer) and the **receipt**. Right: a header at least 340px tall (it grows rather than overflow when a long title and the buttons need more) (⋯ and ×, the title, the fact line: time · Serves n · Keeps n days, and the Planned pill; then Start cooking, Plan, Similar recipes, Open full recipe ↗ only with a source link), the **steps** and **Notes**.
- **Phone** (< 768px): one column that scrolls (the photo with ←, ⋯, × and the "things to buy" sticker; the title, facts, Similar recipes and Full recipe ↗; the receipt; steps; Notes; the footer cards scrolling sideways) and a bar fixed at the bottom with Start cooking and Plan.
- **Receipt** (`recipeReceipt`, on the one matcher's `recipeLines`, so the counts agree with every page): To buy with the servings stepper (one at a time, never under 1), each row with the Ingredient marks (green ✓ on your list, white +; `grocery.toggle`, Undo on the toast), a meta line (amount · the grocery list's store, `namedStoreFor` · ON YOUR LIST · the recipe's group) and the real green sale tag (`SaleTag`, the deal card); **Add to grocery list** (`grocery.addWithUndo`); In your Inventory (blue ✓ header, the amount the recipe uses, "Use soon!" from `expiringItemsIn`); the "Always have" staples. Servings rescale every amount (`lineNeed`) and the numbers in the steps; a food Inventory holds too little of (`shortBy`, only when the units convert) also goes to To buy as "300 G MORE", and its + puts that amount on the list. Tapping a row opens a drawer: why, + Grocery list / Take off, **I have it** (`requestInventoryAdd`, the confirmation sheet; see `CLAUDE.md`), I'm out of this, Open in Inventory.
- **Steps** (`cardSteps`, `stepState`, `nextCurrent`): Cook mode's rail (done ink ✓, current blue and bigger, todo white, a dotted line); tap a step to make it current, tap it again to mark it done and move on ("STEP n OF m", "ALL DONE"); section labels sit between the rows; a step photo is a thumbnail that opens the viewer; the current step lists what it uses (`stepIngredients`, scaled). **Timer pills** (`TimerPill`, `timerPill`): idle "▶ Start 20 min", running "❚❚ 19:42", paused "▶ 19:42", done "✓ Time's up", and ↺ once started; a press never changes the step. They are the shared `useStepTimers` (Cook mode's), so several run at once and carry on in Cook mode, where a timer's time is changed.
- **Similar recipes** (`SimilarView`): swaps the card's content (the frame stays) for "← Back to {title}", the yellow banner with one chip per ingredient (tap to leave it out), the count and the Recipes photo cards with "{n} SHARED". The search is `similarTo` (see Similar recipes). ← Back keeps servings, the current step, running timers and the Prices setting, because they live in the card, not in the view. The footer (`SimilarStrip`) is the same search's top four, as `RecipePhotoCard` `variant="strip"`.
- **⋯ menu** (`OptionsMenu`): Plan around this (the Planner's Main meal), Edit recipe (the form, where tags and **Leftovers keep** are edited: the number of days is `fridgeLifeDays`, saved with the rest of the form, and a blank one means the default of 4 days in `leftoverKeepDays`), View original, Add to my Cookbook / Move to Imported (Toast with Undo), Delete recipe. **Delete is Undo, not a question**: `handleDeleteRecipe` in `App.jsx` takes the recipe and its meals off every page at once and shows the Toast with Undo; the delete is only sent when the toast is gone (`showToast`'s `onGone`: after five seconds, when another message replaces it, when the page is left (`pagehide`, a `keepalive` request) or on logout). If it fails, the recipe comes back and the Toast says so.
- **Notes**: the recipe's own `notes`, saved a moment after typing stops and when the card closes.
- **Prices** wait for Meal pricing (`MEAL_PRICING_READY` in `lib/recipeCard.js` is false): the per-row price and source tags (SALE, FLYER, MEDIAN), the totals (`receiptTotals`), the estimate in the fact line and the Prices switch (`PricesSwitch`) are built but nothing shows; no sample price is anywhere. The switch's saved setting is for the Meal pricing ticket.

Props: `recipe`, `from`, `backLabel`, `onClose`, `allRecipes`, `plannedEntries`, `upcomingEntries`, `kitchen`, `grocery`, `onSelectRecipe`, `onPlan` (`requestPlan`), `onPlanAround`, `onEdit`, `onDelete`, `onRecipeUpdated`, `onRequestInventoryAdd`, `onDeletePantryItem`, `onFinishCooking`, `onNavigate`, `onToast`.

Used by: Recipes (the photo cards), the Planner (search results, a planned meal's Cook), Makeable (a card's photo, title and Cook), Home (the week strip and tonight's Start cooking) and Grocery (a recipe's name in By recipe), and inside the card itself (Similar, the footer).

## Ingredient marks

Every place that lists a recipe's ingredients marks each one with the same round ✓, so it is one piece, not three:

- **Blue ✓**: in your Inventory (it follows Inventory by itself).
- **Green ✓**: not in Inventory, but on your grocery list. Tapping it takes the item off the list and shows the shared Toast with Undo; tapping the + that replaces it puts the item back.
- **White +**: neither; tap to put it on the list. (Inventory wins if an item is both.)

**What counts as "in your Inventory".** One rule everywhere, in `lib/inventoryMatch.js` (see Ingredient-to-Inventory matching): French and English names, varieties and cuts, never leftovers, staples always had. The recipe card's receipt (its "2 things to buy", To buy and In your Inventory, from `recipeLines`, the lines behind `recipeHave`), the planned-meal card's lists, Makeable, the Planner's search, Home and Recipes all come from `recipeHave`, so they always agree with each other and with the finished view's take-out list.

`IngredientMarks` (`components/IngredientMarks.jsx`) draws the "you have" and "to buy" pills for the Planner's planned-meal card. The recipe card draws the same three marks in its receipt: the blue ✓ heading In your Inventory, and the green ✓ / white + beside each thing to buy. All of them read `grocery.isOnList` and call `grocery.toggle`, which `App.jsx` builds once (`toggleGroceryItem`), so Grocery and every mark always agree. A page that shows ingredients gets `grocery` as a prop; do not read the grocery list any other way.

## Slot picker

`SlotPicker` is a small week (7 days by breakfast, lunch, supper) to choose where a recipe goes, with ‹ › to move between weeks, a "Free slot / Replaces ..." line and the confirm button. **`App.jsx` opens it with `requestPlan(recipe)`**, so Plan works the same from any page. It opens on the week the Planner last showed, on the slot chosen as the target if there is one, else the next empty slot. **The week can arrive after the picker opens** (a slow phone), so the starting slot is not chosen once at the start: `pickerSlot` gives what the person picked (or the target) if there is one, else the next empty slot once `loaded`, else the first upcoming slot. Until the week has loaded the grid shows no meals and the confirm button is held, because the picker doesn't yet know what is free (a meal placed on an unseen one would sit on top of it); when it arrives the picker moves to the next empty slot unless the person has already tapped one. `App.jsx` says when it is loaded (`plannerLoadedWeek`). Confirming calls `handlePlanRecipe(recipe, slot, week)`, which places the recipe and shows the toast.

Props: `recipe`, `weekStart`, `entries` (that week's), `loaded` (whether `entries` are that week's yet), `weekend`, `initialSlot`, `onConfirm(slot, week)`, `onClose`.

Used by: the recipe card's Plan (from every page; the picker opens above the card) and the Planner finder's + when no slot is chosen.

## Toast

`Toast` is a dark pill at the bottom of the screen for five seconds, with Undo when the change can be taken back. **`App.jsx` keeps it**: `showToast(message, undo?)`, passed down as `onToast` (Recipes, Inventory) and `actions.toast` (Planner). Do not draw another message. Put what undoing means in `undo`. A toast's five seconds close only that toast (`onClose` checks its `id`), so a message shown just as the last one ends is not taken away with it.

It sits above every card, picker and question (z-index 500), so Undo can be pressed while one is open. `showToast(message, undo?, { onGone })`: `onGone` runs when the toast leaves without Undo (its five seconds, another message replacing it, leaving the page, logout); Undo runs before the toast closes. The recipe card's Delete uses it.

Used by: Planner (add, replace, remove, copy, leftovers, a card dropped on the phone's trash strip, a phone status tap ("marked as leftovers", with Undo putting the old mark back; the card changes at once, and if the save fails it goes back and the Toast says so), Option-drag copies, and "Clear" on a day: one message "Tuesday cleared" whose Undo puts every meal, note and empty card back through `restoreEntry`, leftover marks included, no question first), Recipes (imported), Inventory (added, saved, a frozen leftover moved to the fridge to thaw, with Undo), the grocery ✓ in Ingredient marks (taken off the list), Home's Proteins on sale ("✓ On list" tapped again), Makeable's À acheter ("N items added to the grocery list", Undo takes them off again), the finished view ("Cooked: <recipe>" when it closes, whose Undo puts back the amounts, the leftovers and the cooked mark; "Running low" on a staple, Undo takes it off the list), Inventory leftovers placed on the Planner, and a planned leftover whose day has passed ("1 portion of leftovers taken off", Undo puts the portion back).

## Planner header

`PlannerHeader` is the strip above the Planner board, on a computer and on a phone (designs: `docs/design/riso-v2-planner-header/`, and for the phone `docs/design/riso-v2-planner-mobile-v2/`). The title « Le menu de la **semaine.** » / "This week's **menu.**" has a line to itself, is **never changed by the week**, and wraps to two lines on a phone (the last word in blue). Under it, the controls: ‹, the date pill (blue while the calendar is open), ›.

- **Computer:** the row ends with the yellow sticker, "this week" on the current week and "↩ this week" on another (it goes back). The pill always reads the range, `formatWeekLabel(weekStart)` (`lib/dates.js`): "Oct 5 – 11" / "5 – 11 oct.".
- **Phone:** a small date line sits above the title (`formatWeekRangeLong`: `5 – 11 OCTOBRE`). Props: `weekStart`, `onChangeWeek(weekStart)`, `lastWeekCount`, `onCopyLastWeek`, and for a phone `page`, `onPage(page)`, `weekend`, `onWeekendChange`. The Planner renders it on every size, so there is one header.

## Week calendar

`WeekCalendar` is what the date pill opens. A month drawn as weeks: three dashes under each day (breakfast, lunch, supper; blue when planned), the shown week yellow, today pink. On a computer it sits centred under the pill and a row **picks that week**. On a phone it is a panel from just under the controls row down to 14px from the bottom and the sides, with a caret under the pill, over a dimmed backdrop (the controls stay in front of it); a row does not pick the week. The month arrows only change the month; the arrows next to the pill change the week.

- **Day preview**: on a computer, hovering a day shows a card to the left with its three meals, real recipe photos and the rotated "leftover" tag (`Pill` sticker). On a phone there is no hover: tapping a day shows the same card under that day's row (« Jeudi 8 octobre », « 2 PRÉVUS »), with "Show this week".
- **Weekend (phone only)**: after a dashed rule, the « Fin de semaine » section is the shared `WeekendSettings` (`inCalendar`): the switch, `DAYS · summary`, seven one-letter day buttons, « la veille au souper », the presets. Every change is saved at once, the same setting as the computer's tag menu.
- **Footer**: "Go to this week" (yellow on a phone) and "↺ Copy last week". Copy fills only empty slots, shows the toast with Undo (`actions.copyLastWeek`) and the button says "✓ Copied" for a moment. It is greyed out when last week is empty.

The data is `GET /api/planner/dates` (`{ date, meals }` for each planned day; each meal has `mealType`, `title`, `placeholder`, `photoUrl`, `isLeftover`). Its helpers are in `lib/plannerCalendar.js` (`monthWeeks`, `inWeek`, `weekOf`).

Props: `weekStart`, `onPick(dateKey)`, `onThisWeek`, `onCopyLastWeek` (resolves with how many meals it copied), `canCopy`, `weekend`, `onWeekendChange` (a phone), `onClose`.

Used by: `PlannerHeader`.

## Weekend settings

`WeekendSettings` (in `components/PlannerExtras.jsx`) is the one set of controls for the weekend: a switch to show it, any days in any order, a switch for the evening before, and the three presets. Each change goes to `onChange(weekend)` and is saved at once for the account (`handleSaveWeekend` in `App.jsx`, `lib/weekend.js` for the model). `WeekendMenu` wraps it in the popover under the "WEEKEND ▾" tag on a computer; with `inCalendar` it is the « Fin de semaine » section of a phone's week calendar (the title is the switch's label, one letter per day, a `DAYS · summary` line). Do not build a second weekend control.

## Planner legend

`PlannerLegend` is the row under the board on a computer: planned meal, ingredients on hand (blue outline), leftovers (yellow tag), cooked (the ink "✓ Cooked" tag "I cooked this" puts on a meal), note, empty slot, today and, when the board shows one, the weekend ("Fin de semaine", without the list of days). ## Planner board

`PlannerBoard` draws the week for the Planner on a computer: all seven days in view. On a phone the Planner draws `PlannerBoardPhone` instead (design: `docs/design/riso-v2-planner-mobile-v2/`), which reuses `PlannerBoard`'s card pieces (`PlannerMealCard`, `PlannerNoteCard`, `computeStaleLeftoverIds`) and the same drop slots (`day-<d>-<meal>`), so cards, drag, swap, Clear and the toast are the same pieces everywhere.

**Phone board.** Three days in view on a track (`.pmb-track`, 104px columns, 8px apart, about 40px of the next day peeking) that slides with `translateX(trackX(page, dragDx))` (`lib/plannerPhone.js`; pages start at days 0, 3 and 4 so every page has three days, `PHONE_PAGE_STARTS`). The meal names (Déjeuner, Dîner, Souper) are **not on the track**: they sit at x = 14 above each row and follow the rows when an inline card pushes them down. All the numbers (row tops, the board's height, the card's place, the notch, the weekend band's outline) come from `boardGeometry`, `notchLeft` and `weekendBands`, so everything agrees on where a row is. The page is owned by `Planner` and changed by the header's stickers, a swipe along the board (past 50px; pointer events with `touch-action: pan-y`; ignored while a card is being dragged) and holding a dragged card near the left or right of the screen (`useDndMonitor`). A new week starts on the page with today (Mon–Wed for another week), without sliding.

A card is 104 × 104 (photo 50px, name, time), with a 3px blue border for "already have", a 3px yellow border and the « restes » tag for leftovers, no round ✓ and no ×; a note says « ✎ NOTE » over its text; "Nothing planned" is a plain solid card; past days are faded and cannot be opened. « Vider » is a tiny link under each future day with something planned. The weekend is a dotted pink band (an SVG outline, an L when it takes in the evening before); its settings live in the calendar. The open slot or meal's card is the board's `inline` prop (see Slot card), measured so the rows below move.

The computer's board: seven days by three meals, the weekend, cards and "Clear" as on every size (`lib/weekend.js`). ## Pictures in held or dragged things

One rule in `index.css` (`.press-hold :is(img, .picture-stand-in) { pointer-events: none }`), not one per page. A picture can be replaced under a finger: one that will not load, or loads late, becomes a placeholder (Inventory's `ItemPhoto`, the recipe form's `.re-photo-broken`). A touch whose element has left the page is lost: the browser takes it for a page scroll and the press-and-hold or drag is cancelled. With every picture inside the piece out of the way, the touch always lands on the piece itself, which stays; a tap on the picture still opens the piece.

- **Who carries `press-hold`:** Inventory's items when they can drag (`InventoryItem`), a Planner card (`PlannerMealCard`, on a computer and on a phone), a finder result and a Leftovers result when they can drag (`ResultCard`, `LeftoverResultCard`), and the recipe form's photo tiles (`SortablePhoto`). Grocery's rows and Store mode's rows hold no picture, and Makeable's cards and the recipe card are tapped, never held or dragged.
- **A new piece you can hold or drag** that shows a picture adds `press-hold` to its outer element. A placeholder that stands in for a picture (anything but an `<img>`) adds `picture-stand-in`. Do not write a `pointer-events: none` for one page's photo.
- **Today only Inventory (and the recipe form's tiles) take a picture out of the page when it fails;** `RecipePhoto` only hides it, so the Planner and the finder keep their `<img>`. The rule is what keeps that true if it ever changes.
- **Tests:** `e2e/picture-fails.js` holds a picture's request until the test lets it fail, so it fails while the finger is down (during the hold, or while the piece is carried). `e2e/inventory-compact-tiles.spec.js` (Inventory, fails without the rule) and `e2e/planner-mobile.spec.js` (a Planner card) use it.

## Home's two screens

Home (`components/Home.jsx`; design `docs/design/riso-v2-home-two-screens/`, README first) is **one scroller holding two screens**, with a column of dots on the right edge. Only the layout is the design's; every card is the one it was. **Screen 1** is the greeting, the hero beside the Grocery card, and the week strip. **Screen 2** is Proteins on sale, Makeable now (only when it has something) and Use it up, in the same grid as before (three across, or two when Makeable steps aside, one column on a phone).

- **The shell.** `App.jsx` adds `is-home` to `.app` while the Home tab shows, and only then. That makes `.app` exactly `100dvh` tall (a flex column, `overflow: hidden`): the header keeps its own height and `.home-page` takes the rest (`flex: 1 1 0; min-height: 0`). **No header height is written down**: it is 84 px on a computer, 140 px when the tabs wrap onto a second row (about 1000 px wide) and 108 px on a phone, and it moves with the language and the fonts. So the page never scrolls on Home and every other page keeps its own shell. The design's header has no gap below it, so on Home the header's bottom margin is 0 and the screens' top padding (24 px, 18 on a phone) is the gap.
- **The scroller** (`.home-scroller`): `overflow-y: auto`, `position: relative` (the screens' `offsetTop` counts from it), `scroll-behavior: smooth`, no scrollbar. **One scroller runs through both screens**: a screen taller than the window is not a scroller of its own. A screen is `min-height: 100%` of it (not the README's `container-type: size` and `100cqh`: the same result, with no container query and nothing that could shut the Proteins card in under its scrim, which is on `<body>` at z-index 60; the open card is z-index 61). Side padding is on the screens (40 px, 18 on a phone), so the scroller is full width and the dots sit at the edge.
- **How it moves: `scrollMode`** (`lib/homeScreens.js`, unit-tested). **A computer scrolls freely at every size**, with the mouse wheel and the trackpad, from anywhere on the page (no snap, no flick stop). **A touch screen** (`TOUCH_SCREEN`, `(pointer: coarse)`, followed by `useMediaQuery`) **snaps where both screens fit**, a tablet: `screensFit` is true when both screens are no taller than the scroller (Home measures it with a `ResizeObserver`; the sizes move with the window and with a card's data) and Home sets `data-snap="on"`, which turns on `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory`. **A phone** (a touch screen where they don't fit) scrolls freely with the flick stop (`data-flick-stop="on"`, below). Why a computer never snaps (Matt's test of Oct 13, reproduced in Chromium at 1280 × 720, 1440 × 900, 1920 × 1080 and 1000 × 800): with the snap, a trackpad scroll shorter than half a screen springs back and a slow one wobbles hundreds of times without leaving screen 1 (the test tool's wheel events reach Chrome as a trackpad's pixel steps; a real mouse notch in Chrome may jump a whole screen, which could not be checked). And in a screen taller than the window a snap point is a wall: ordinary swipes and wheel ticks bounce back from it, and only a drag of more than half a screen or a flick gets across, so a phone doesn't snap either.
- **The flick stop, on a phone.** **To turn it off, set `HOME_FLICK_STOP` to `false` in `lib/homeScreens.js`: that one line brings back free scrolling on a phone, and the browser tests that need it skip themselves.** With it on (Matt's iPhone, Oct 13: a flick from screen 1 flew past the start of screen 2), a flick never carries the scroller across the join between the screens. Going down, a flick that set off above the top of screen 2 stops with that top at the top of the scroller; going up, one that set off below the end of screen 1 stops with that end at the bottom of the scroller (so from deep in screen 2 it runs past the top of screen 2 and stops at the join). Inside a screen it runs free, a flick that runs out before the join rests where it stops (nothing pulls it), and a finger on the screen is never stopped, so dragging across the join and reading stay free: no wall. The dots still jump to the top of each screen. The rule is `flickStop` (`lib/homeScreens.js`, unit-tested: where a coast stops, from where it began, where it is and its last step, stopping one step early rather than one late); `useHomeFlickStop` (`hooks/`) watches the scroller while `data-flick-stop="on"` (`scrollMode` "flick-stop": a phone, with the switch on). A coast is what follows a lifted finger after a swipe (a tap doesn't count), until 200 ms pass with no scroll. A stop sets the scroller on the join and holds it there until the coasting is over, putting back only movement past the join in the coast's own direction: it turns scrolling off (`overflow-y: hidden`, which ends the coasting) until the scroller has been still for 100 ms; on a slow device the coasting can run on for a few frames before that takes hold, and what it moves past the join is put back (tested with the processor slowed four times). **It only ever catches a coast, never a scroll the person or the app asks for:** a finger or the mouse pressed anywhere, a key (not Shift, Ctrl, Alt or Cmd alone) or the focus moving ends the coast and the hold at once (so a dot, a link or a card, and a `scrollIntoView` after a tap go where they're asked), and anything that moves the scroller other than the coast itself ends the hold and stays where it went. **It listens to touch only. The wheel and trackpad part (PR #156: a stop at the join for the wheel, held while it kept turning, with the wheel events cancelled and put back) and its tests were removed, not switched off**: on Matt's computer (Oct 13) it made the trackpad janky and Home stopped at the join for as long as you kept scrolling, so the top of screen 1 and the end of screen 2 couldn't be reached in one go. No wheel listener is left on Home. Tested in Chromium with real touch flicks at 390 × 664 and 390 × 844 in French with all three cards on screen 2. The test checks the rule, not glide distances: a long or fast flick that would cross the join stops exactly on it (started 400 to 740 px from the join, which it would otherwise overshoot by 2.5 times or more), a gentle one that runs out first rests where it stops (started at least 550 px away; it glides about 220), and inside a screen it runs free. The finger is in the screens' left margin, where only Home scrolls, and its touch events carry exact timestamps, so the speed the browser reads is the same on a busy machine. Safari (WebKit) could not be tested: its download is blocked in the build machine, Playwright can't send touch swipes to WebKit, and WebKit on Linux doesn't coast like an iPhone; turning `overflow` off is the known way to stop iOS momentum, but it is checked on Matt's iPhone, not in a test.

## Pending saves

A change shows on screen at once and is saved in the background. A page opened right after (the Grocery tab, Store mode, the recipe card's ✓) used to ask the server for the data before that save had landed, and showed the old one. So `request` in `api.js` keeps every save that can change the grocery list in `lib/pendingSaves.js` until it ends, and **a read of grocery data or of upcoming meals first waits for those saves, and is read again (at most twice) if a save started or ended while it was on its way**, because that answer can be older than the change. This is built once, in the data code every page uses: a page reads through `api` as it always did and does nothing extra. Do not wait for saves in a page.

What the grocery list is made of, which is what counts (`saveArea` and `readArea` in `lib/pendingSaves.js` say which request is which, with tests):

- **Saves:** every `/grocery…` change; **every Planner save** (`/planner…`: placing, moving, removing, noting, copying, marking a meal; the grocery list is built from the planned meals, so a read right after planning a meal must see it); and a recipe **edited or deleted by its id** (`PUT` or `DELETE /recipes/:id`: its ingredients are on the list through the meals that use it). A recipe link being read (`/recipes/scrape`) or a pasted list (`/recipes/parse-ingredients`) saves nothing and can be slow, so it is not one.
- **Reads that wait:** every `/grocery…` read and `GET /planner/upcoming` (`api.listPlannerUpcoming`, which Grocery, Home and `App.jsx` read the meals from). The Planner's own week (`GET /planner?week=`) does not wait.

One more piece is for a page that loads several things together and then puts them on screen (the Grocery list): `savesVersion("grocery")` changes when a save starts and when it ends. If it changed while the page was loading, the page loads again (`GroceryList.jsx`, at most twice more) instead of putting the older answers over the person's change.

**Saves in order.** Quick taps on one item used to send their saves together, and the server could finish them in another order (the box said queued, a reload said bought). `saveInOrder({ area })` (`lib/saveInOrder.js`, with tests) queues each item's saves one after another; `run(key, save)` resolves with `{ ok, latest }`, so only the newest tap undoes its change when its save fails, and with `area` the whole queue counts as a save in progress here from the moment of the tap, so a read waits for saves that haven't started yet. The grocery checkbox (`setStep` in `GroceryList.jsx`) uses it; a browser test slows the first save and checks the last tap wins.

Used by: every `api.listGrocery…` read, every grocery save (`addGroceryExtra`, `deleteGroceryExtra`, `setGroceryOverride`, the checks (one item's in order, above), the stores), every Planner save (`placeOnPlanner`, `removeFromPlanner`, `updatePlannerEntry`, `markSlotBlank`, `setPlannerEntryNote`, `copyPlannerWeek`), `updateRecipe` and `deleteRecipe`, including Home's Proteins on sale (its Add to list, and Home's grocery card reading again after it).

## Quebec day

A date the app shows or counts is a day in Quebec, not a moment on the device's clock or the server's (UTC): at 11 p.m. in Quebec it is already the next day in UTC. `lib/quebecDay.js` is the one rule (`America/Toronto`); `server/src/lib/quebecDay.js` is the same code for the server (the AI limit's "today", the leftover keep-time bar), and `server/src/lib/quebecDay.test.js` checks the two agree. Do not count days with `Date.now()` or the device's date.

- `quebecDay(moment)` is the Quebec day ("2026-10-12"); `addDays`, `daysBetween` do the arithmetic on days.
- **A use-by date is a day** (`expiryDay`). A day typed or picked is sent as "2026-10-16" and the database keeps it as midnight UTC, and that is the day it says (so dates already saved read right). Any other moment (a USDA shelf life worked out from a time) is the Quebec day it falls on. Never cut a saved date with `slice(0, 10)`: that is the UTC day.
- **Days left** is `daysUntil` (`lib/pantryInventory.js`): Quebec calendar days from today, 0 on the use-by day itself, the same at 9 a.m. and 11 p.m. Inventory, Home, Makeable, the finder, the recipe card, Flyers (`daysLeft` for a flyer's end day) and Grocery's sale days (and which flyer deals are still running there) all count it. **The use-by day itself is not expired:** the count is 0 and everything says today (Inventory's card "expires today" / « expire aujourd'hui » from `expiry.today`, the add form's tag "Today" / « Aujourd'hui » from `days.today`, Home's Use it up "today!" / « aujourd'hui! », the finder's leftover card and Cook with chip, the recipe card's "Expires today"), the item keeps a short pink stub of life left (`lineForDays`) and is counted under "to use soon", and Home still lists it. **Expired is only below 0**, the day after: the Expired tag and pink-tinted card, the form's Expired tag and note, and the expired count. The Planner's "past fridge life" sticker (`leftoverIsStale`) says a leftover meal is late only when its day comes after the use-by day, so a meal on the use-by day is fine. Every rule that tells expired from not expired is `days < 0` (`expiryTag`, `lineForDays`, `useByKind` in `lib/inventoryForm.js`); do not write `days <= 0`.
- **Today in the Inventory form** is the Quebec day (`dateFromDays`, `daysFromDate`), so the use-by chips ("3 days", "1 week") land on the right day on any device.
- Leftovers: `leftoverExpiry` gives the use-by day for `fridgeLifeDays` or 4 days from the Quebec day it is put away.

`ThemeSwitch` (`components/ThemeSwitch.jsx`) is the round ☀ / ☾ button (☀ in dark, ☾ in light; « Thème clair » / « Thème sombre » from `theme.toLight` / `theme.toDark`). - **Last step:** Next says I cooked this (`cooked.button`, « Je l'ai cuisiné ») and Up next is hidden; it opens the finished view over Cook mode (`onFinish`, through the recipe card to `openCooked` in `App.jsx`). Cook mode stays under it: "Back to step 1" there comes back to step 1, × and "Back to the app" close both.
- **Closing:** × or Escape closes it at once. The step timers belong to the recipe card (`useStepTimers` lives in `RecipeDetailModal`), so a timer carries on there and in Cook mode when it is reopened; nothing is asked.
- **Which ingredients a step uses** (`stepIngredients`, matching in `lib/ingredientMatch.js`): a name matches when one of its words is a whole word of the step. Short names count ("egg", "oil", « ail », « riz »), and a word inside another word does not ("rice" is not in "price", "oil" is not in "boil"). In a longer name a word of three letters or fewer counts only when it is a known food (`SHORT_FOODS`, or any word in the French / English pairs), so the "dry" of "dry white wine" and the "all" of "all-purpose flour" never match a step that merely says "dry" or "all". Singular and plural match either way ("tomatoes" / "tomato", « oignon » / « oignons »), with the grocery list's singular rules (`singularize`) and accents ignored. The other language counts: the one-word French / English pairs the flyers use (`PHRASES` in `lib/bilingual.js`: « ail » is garlic, « riz » is rice) and whole phrases through `frenchToEnglish` (« pommes de terre » are potatoes). A colour, a size, "fresh", "ground" or a little word (« de », "the") never matches on its own, so "red onion" is found by "onion" and not by "red pepper"; « thé » / "the" and « maïs » / « mais » (but) are kept apart. One matching, so a change to it changes "For this step" and the prep page's step tags together.
- **Also:** ← → (and a swipe on a touch screen) move between steps, Space starts or pauses the timer, Keep screen on holds a wake lock, amounts and the numbers in the text scale with the servings.

## Ingredient-to-Inventory matching

`lib/inventoryMatch.js` is **the one matcher in the app**: it says which Inventory item is a recipe ingredient, for every page that decides "have it or not" and for the finished view's take-out list (`buildTakeOut`). Do not compare Inventory with a recipe any other way (`core()` in `lib/similarRecipes.js` is only for comparing recipes with each other, the grocery list and flyer deals). Two names are the same food once both are read in English: French names count (the flyers' word pairs in `lib/bilingual.js` plus kitchen words: « Poulet », « Lait 2 % », « ail », « pommes de terre »), food words in any order, prep and descriptor words left out ("whole", "2%", "unsalted", "boneless"); a colour or variety ("yellow", "basmati", "greek") and a cut ("thighs") only separate two foods when both names give one; a stock, powder, paste, sauce or juice is never the food it is made of; a staple only matches a staple; leftovers never match. French staples and spices count as staples too (« sel », « poivre », « huile végétale », « farine », « cassonade », « poudre à pâte », « bicarbonate de soude », « fécule de maïs », « origan »...), so does "salt and pepper" / « sel et poivre » on one line, and a bell pepper (« poivrons ») is a vegetable, never the spice. Several items for one ingredient: the one still good with the soonest use-by first, the rest from the next. `buildTakeOut` scales the recipe's amounts to the servings cooked, adds up an ingredient listed twice, converts to the item's unit (weights with weights, volumes with volumes, counts with counts, `convertAmount`) and asks when it can't. Unit tests: `lib/inventoryMatch.test.js`.

What the pages use (`kitchen` is App's `{ inventory, customStaples, excludedStaples }`, built once in `App.jsx` and passed down):

- `ingredientHave(name, kitchen)`: "staple", "have" or "need" for one ingredient. The recipe card's ✓ beside each line.
- `recipeHave(recipe, kitchen)`: `{ have, buy, missing, totalCount, matchedCount, missingCount }`, one line per food in the recipe's order (two lines are one food when each covers the other: "garlic cloves" and "minced garlic"), named as the recipe writes it, staples left out. The one count: the recipe card's receipt (`recipeLines`, the same lines, with `lineNeed` and `shortBy` for the amounts and what you'd run short of at other servings), the planned-meal card (`IngredientMarks`), the Planner's search and Makeable (through `rankRecipesForTray` in `lib/plannerSuggestions.js`, then `findRecipes` and `makeableSections`), Home's Makeable now and tonight's card (`makeableNow` in `lib/homeWeek.js`), the Recipes page's "makeable now" count and Fewest missing sort, and the recipe form's preview.
- `recipeLines(recipe, kitchen)`: the lines behind `recipeHave`, with each line's ingredients, and the staples apart. The recipe card's receipt (`recipeReceipt` in `lib/recipeCard.js`) is built on it, with `lineNeed(ingredients, scale)` (one food's amounts added up in the first one's unit) and `shortBy(need, name, inventory)` (how much more to buy when your Inventory items, still good, hold too little; null when it can't be told, so it never guesses).
- `expiringItemsIn(recipe, inventory, plannerEntries, recipes, days)`: the "uses expiring" rule (the recipe card's "use soon", the Makeable card's pink strip, the Recipes count). The finder's "Expiring soon" and its ranking use `expiringSoon` and `recipeUsesItem`.
- Cook with: `recipeUsesItem(recipe, itemName)` finds the recipes using a pick (a staple also by its name inside the recipe's, "smoked paprika"), and `foodKey` lists each food once on the shelves; leftovers are not on the shelves.

The answers are kept per Inventory (a `WeakMap` on the array) until it, the staples or the day change. Unit tests: `lib/inventoryMatch.test.js`, and `lib/oneMatcher.test.js` checks one recipe gives the same answer on every page, in English and French; `e2e/one-matcher.spec.js` checks it in the app on a computer and at 390px in French.

## Reorder sections

`components/ReorderSections.jsx` is **the one way to move a whole section**: a store on Grocery's By store and a shelf on Inventory. Do not build a second one; a new list of sections to reorder uses it too. A page gives each section `useReorderSection({ id, data })` (its `ref`, the `style` that slides or carries it, `lifted`, and its `grip`), draws the ⠿ with `ReorderGrip` (a span in Grocery's heading, a button on a shelf), and wraps the sections in `ReorderList` (`ids`, and a `strategy`). What a drop does is the page's own: Grocery's `handleDragEnd` saves the store order, Inventory's `useReorderDrop` saves the layout.

- **How it feels (the same on both pages).** A mouse picks the ⠿ up after 6px of movement; a finger needs no hold (`REORDER_MOUSE`, `REORDER_TOUCH`: the ⠿ is `touch-action: none`, so a touch that starts on it never scrolls the page and can only be moving the section). The section itself lifts and follows the pointer (`.reorder-lifted`: 90% opacity, shadow, above the others), the others slide out of the way (dnd-kit's 200 ms transition), and it lands in the place of the section it is dropped on, with no extra animation. The new order shows at once and is then saved (Grocery's `saveStoreOrder`; Inventory's `handleSaveInventoryLayout`, which puts the old layout back if the save fails). **Item rows keep their own press-and-hold on both pages.** The page scrolls near the top and bottom of the screen (dnd-kit's auto-scroll).
- **Sections of different widths** (Inventory's shelves: Full, Half, Third on a 12-column grid). `gridSortingStrategy` (`lib/reorderGrid.js`, unit-tested) works the new layout out the way the CSS grid will (fill the row from the left, wrap a section that does not fit, a row as tall as its tallest) and slides every shelf there at its own width, so the preview is the result. `sectionCollision` says which shelf the pointer is over: the one under it, else the nearest by its middle. One section per row (Grocery's stores, a phone) uses `verticalListSortingStrategy`, which is what Grocery has always used, with the nearest-middle rule in its own `collisionDetection`.
- **Two drag setups.** Grocery has its own `DndContext` (`useReorderSensors`: mouse 6px, touch 250 ms hold with 8px of give). Inventory's shelves sit in the app's one setup (`App.jsx`), because the items inside them drag between shelves there. Its one pointer sensor is `GripAwarePointerSensor`: everything else is picked up after the 200 ms hold, a press that starts on a grip (`data-reorder-grip`) the grip's way. (Two pointer sensors cannot share a setup: the second replaces the first.) `App.jsx`'s collision rule sends a `type: "shelf"` drag to `sectionCollision`, `handleDragEnd` leaves it to Inventory, and the floating-copy layer (`DragOverlay`) is left out while a shelf is carried, because with it up dnd-kit does not move the real shelf.
- **A shelf is also where an item is dropped.** It is one `useSortable` for both (`inv-shelf-<id>`); the dashed "drop here" look only shows for an item being carried (`inventoryItemId`), and the shelves do not slide for one.

## Recently removed

Anything that leaves Inventory waits in Recently removed (« Récemment retirés ») for 7 Quebec days, then goes for good. The Inventory row itself is deleted as it always was, and a copy waits in its own table (`PantryRemovedItem`, migration `20261013120000_pantry_removed_items`, add-only, deleted with its account), so `PantryInventoryItem` still means "in stock" and **nothing that reads Inventory can count a removed item**: not the matcher, I have it, Makeable, Use it up, the counts or the summary line.

- **What goes there, and how it's labelled** (`server/src/lib/removedItems.js`, `recordRemoved`, written in the same transaction as the delete): Used up and Tossed (the bulk bar and the pop-up, `POST /consume`, with the log line's id), Deleted (the pop-up's Remove, `DELETE /:id`), Used up for the recipe card's "I'm out of this" (`DELETE /:id?how=consumed`) and for a planned leftover whose day passed (`leftovers/settle`), and Cooked for "I cooked this" taking an item to zero (`POST /take-out`). Taking back an add (the add form's Undo, the finished view's Undo of its leftovers: `DELETE /:id?forget=1`) is not kept. A partial take-out never leaves Inventory.
- **The list:** `GET /api/pantry-inventory/removed` deletes entries older than 7 Quebec days, then lists the rest, newest first. The page reads it when it opens and again whenever a removal, an Undo or a Put back has landed (`removedChanged` in `lib/removedItems.js`, called by those calls in `api.js`; `hooks/useRemovedItems.js`). It is the shared `RemovedStrip`, collapsed under the shelves, shown only when something is in it: each row is the name struck through, "USED UP · YESTERDAY" (`removedHowLabel`, `removedWhenLabel`, Quebec days) and « Remettre » / Put back.
- **Put back** (`POST /removed/:id/put-back`, `handlePutBackRemoved` in `App.jsx`) restores the row as it was (`restoreRow`, the same code as Undo's `put-back`: same id, shelf, amount, use-by date, photo, leftover link; a shelf that no longer exists puts it in the Pantry), drops its "used up" / "tossed" log line, and takes it off the list. Like Undo it doesn't open the confirmation sheet (`CLAUDE.md`). The existing Undo toasts (`put-back`) also take the rows they restore off the list.

## Where things live in `App.jsx`

`App.jsx` owns the data and the shared pieces above: `openRecipeCard`, `requestPlan`, `showToast`. A page gets them as props. A new page that needs a recipe opened, a recipe planned or a message shown uses these and does not make its own.
