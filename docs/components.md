**Before building any new piece of UI, check this list and reuse or extend what is here. Never build a second version of something that already exists.** When you add or change a shared piece, update its entry in this file in the same change.

Everything below is one piece used by every page that shows the same thing. The page around it only passes data and what happens next.

## Recipe card

`RecipeDetailModal` (design: `docs/design/riso-v2-recipe-card/`; pieces in `RecipeCardParts.jsx`, logic in `lib/recipeCard.js` with tests) is **the one card for a recipe**. `App.jsx` renders it once and opens it with `openRecipeCard(recipeOrId, from?)` (`from` is the box of the card it grows out of), over the page you are on: the back pill says that page ("← Planner", `pageLabel`), and closing goes back to it. It replaced the recipe pop-out: every page opens this card in one tap.

- **Computer** (≥ 768px): two columns, `440px | 1fr` (under 1180px: `360px | 1fr`, three footer cards, and the timer pills under the step text), with "Uses the same ingredients" along the bottom. Left: the photo (340px, ← back pill, "1 / 3 photos", ← → flip, tap for the full-size viewer) and the **receipt**. Right: a header at least 340px tall (it grows rather than overflow when a long title and the buttons need more) (⋯ and ×, the title, the fact line: time · Serves n · Keeps n days, and the Planned pill; then Start cooking, Plan, Similar recipes, Open full recipe ↗ only with a source link), the **steps** and **Notes**.
- **Phone** (< 768px): one column that scrolls (the photo with ←, ⋯, × and the "things to buy" sticker; the title, facts, Similar recipes and Full recipe ↗; the receipt; steps; Notes; the footer cards scrolling sideways) and a bar fixed at the bottom with Start cooking and Plan.
- **Receipt** (`recipeReceipt`, on the one matcher's `recipeLines`, so the counts agree with every page): To buy with the servings stepper (one at a time, never under 1), each row with the Ingredient marks (green ✓ on your list, white +; `grocery.toggle`, Undo on the toast), a meta line (amount · the grocery list's store, `namedStoreFor` · ON YOUR LIST · the recipe's group) and the real green sale tag (`SaleTag`, the deal card); **Add to grocery list** (`grocery.addWithUndo`); In your Inventory (blue ✓ header, the amount the recipe uses, "Use soon!" from `expiringItemsIn`). Servings rescale every amount (`lineNeed`) and the numbers in the steps; a food Inventory holds too little of (`shortBy`, only when the units convert) also goes to To buy as "300 G MORE", and its + puts that amount on the list. Tapping a row opens a drawer: why, + Grocery list / Take off, **I have it** (`requestInventoryAdd`, the confirmation sheet; see `CLAUDE.md`), **I have it as…** (« Je l'ai comme… »: you keep it under another name, so it links the two names and adds nothing to Inventory; see Taught names), I'm out of this, Open in Inventory. A blue row that is blue only because of a taught name says so in its drawer ("Counts because you said Soy sauce counts as Tamari.") with Remove (`linkBehind`).
- **Steps** (`cardSteps`, `stepState`, `nextCurrent`): Cook mode's rail (done ink ✓, current blue and bigger, todo white, a dotted line); tap a step to make it current, tap it again to mark it done and move on ("STEP n OF m", "ALL DONE"); section labels sit between the rows; a step photo is a thumbnail that opens the viewer; the current step lists what it uses (`stepIngredients`, scaled). **Timer pills** (`TimerPill`, `timerPill`): idle "▶ Start 20 min", running "❚❚ 19:42", paused "▶ 19:42", done "✓ Time's up", and ↺ once started; a press never changes the step. They are the shared `useStepTimers` (Cook mode's), so several run at once and carry on in Cook mode, where a timer's time is changed.
- **Similar recipes** (`SimilarView`): swaps the card's content (the frame stays) for "← Back to {title}", the yellow banner with one chip per ingredient (tap to leave it out), the count and the Recipes photo cards with "{n} SHARED". The search is `similarTo` (see Similar recipes). ← Back keeps servings, the current step, running timers and the Prices setting, because they live in the card, not in the view. The footer (`SimilarStrip`) is the same search's top four, as `RecipePhotoCard` `variant="strip"`.
- **⋯ menu** (`OptionsMenu`): Plan around this (the Planner's Main meal), Edit recipe (the form, where tags and **Leftovers keep** are edited: the number of days is `fridgeLifeDays`, saved with the rest of the form, and a blank one means the default of 4 days in `leftoverKeepDays`), View original, Add to my Cookbook / Move to Imported (Toast with Undo), **Hide from Makeable / Show in Makeable** (« Masquer de Faisable » / « Afficher dans Faisable »: `onSetMakeableHidden`, which is `handleSetMakeableHidden` in `App.jsx`; it saves `hiddenFromMakeable` through `api.updateRecipe`, the recipe store takes the saved recipe so every page agrees, and the Toast has Undo; see Makeable now rule), Delete recipe. **Delete is Undo, not a question**: `handleDeleteRecipe` in `App.jsx` hides the recipe in the recipe store, which takes it and its meals off every page at once (the Planner, Home, Grocery, Makeable), and shows the Toast with Undo (`showRecipe`); the delete is only sent when the toast is gone (`showToast`'s `onGone`: after five seconds, when another message replaces it, when the page is left (`pagehide`, a `keepalive` request) or on logout). If it fails, the recipe comes back and the Toast says so.
- **Notes**: the recipe's own `notes`, saved a moment after typing stops and when the card closes.

## Ingredient marks

## Toast

`Toast` is a dark pill at the bottom of the screen for five seconds, with Undo when the change can be taken back. **`App.jsx` keeps it**: `showToast(message, undo?)`, passed down as `onToast` (Recipes, Inventory, the Feedback inbox) and `actions.toast` (Planner). Do not draw another message. Put what undoing means in `undo`. A toast's five seconds close only that toast (`onClose` checks its `id`), so a message shown just as the last one ends is not taken away with it.

It sits above every card, picker and question (z-index 500), so Undo can be pressed while one is open. `showToast(message, undo?, { onGone, replace, variant })`: `onGone` runs when the toast leaves without Undo (its five seconds, another message replacing it, leaving the page, logout); Undo runs before the toast closes. The recipe card's Delete uses it.

**Look and motion** (design `docs/design/riso-v2-grocery-check-feedback/` §6): the pill comes in with an 8 px rise and a 160 ms fade the first time, a new message in the same pill cross-fades its text (120 ms, linear) and restarts the five seconds (which are real time: slow motion never stretches them), and it leaves with a 160 ms fade. A message is a text, or `{ name, rest }` for an item's name (bold) and its "· where it went" (which never breaks inside), as Grocery's checks show. **The name matters more than the verb:** when both don't fit on one line the verb drops under the name (the pill stays 48 px tall), and the name is cut with … only when it alone is too long; a browser test checks « Papier d'aluminium » and "Aluminum foil" at 360 and 390 px, in French and English, on the list and in Store mode. `replace: true` says the message follows another quickly (Grocery's one toast per tap): the same pill keeps its place instead of re-entering. `variant: "store-dark" | "store-light"` is Store mode's pill (24 px from the bottom, a bigger Undo; paper-coloured on the dark screen). On a phone it sits 96 px up, which clears Grocery's docked cart. Taps go through its body to the page under it (`pointer-events: none`); only Undo, a 44 px tall hit area around its 28 px pill, takes one. For reduced motion it enters and leaves with opacity only.

Used by: Planner (add, replace, remove, copy, leftovers, a card dropped on the phone's trash strip, a phone status tap ("marked as leftovers", with Undo putting the old mark back; the card changes at once, and if the save fails it goes back and the Toast says so), Option-drag copies, and "Clear" on a day: one message "Tuesday cleared" whose Undo puts every meal, note and empty card back through `restoreEntry`, leftover marks included, no question first), Recipes (imported), Inventory (added, saved, a frozen leftover moved to the fridge to thaw, with Undo), the grocery ✓ in Ingredient marks (taken off the list), Home's Proteins on sale ("✓ On list" tapped again), Makeable's À acheter ("N items added to the grocery list", Undo takes them off again), the finished view ("Cooked: <recipe>" when it closes, whose Undo puts back the amounts, the leftovers and the cooked mark), Inventory leftovers placed on the Planner, and a planned leftover whose day has passed ("1 portion of leftovers taken off", Undo puts the portion back), a sent Feedback ("Thanks!" / « Merci! », no Undo), and a taught name ("Rice wine vinegar now counts as Rice vinegar", "… no longer counts as …", whose Undo takes it back or puts it back).

## Keep inside the window

`lib/placeInWindow.js` is the one rule for a box that floats next to something and has to stay on screen. Everything is in window coordinates (`getBoundingClientRect()`), with a 12px margin (`WINDOW_MARGIN`) from every side. `placeBeside({ anchor, size, view, prefer, gap, align, limit })` puts the box beside the anchor on the `prefer` side (`"right"` or `"left"`, 16px apart); if it does not fit there, on the other side; then `keepInside` shifts it so no side leaves the window. `align: "bottom"` lines its bottom up with the anchor's instead of its top. `limit` is the `{ left, right }` it has to fit between to count as fitting (the Planner's slot cards use the board's edges; with none it is the window less the margin). `centerOn(anchor, width, view)` is the left of a box centred on the anchor and shifted inside sideways (the week calendar's panel under the date pill). `viewSize()` is the window without a scrollbar.

Used by: the Planner's slot card and planned-meal card (`useBesideSlot`), the week calendar's panel and its day card. `lib/placeInWindow.test.js` covers the four edges, the flip and every window width from 768. Browser test: `e2e/window-edges.spec.js` (the calendar and its card at 1280, 1100, 900 and 768px in English and French, and in a short scrolled window).

## Where a page starts

`lib/pageScroll.js` `scrollPageToTop({ smooth })` is the only thing that sends a whole page to its top, and `App.jsx` is its only caller.

- **A page opens at its top, Home on its first screen.** `App.jsx` has a `useLayoutEffect` on `tab`, so it runs for every way the page can change (a header tab, the app name, Back and Forward on the admin pages, Home's buttons, "See in Flyers", the recipe form's "Recipes"), before the new page is drawn, and jumps (no animation). Home does not scroll the page: its two screens scroll inside `.home-scroller`, which is made again each time Home opens, so it starts on screen 1; the reset also sends it back if it somehow is not.
- **Tapping the tab you are already on** (`goToTab` → `showTab` when `next === tab`, the app name on Home too) scrolls that page smoothly back to the top (instantly for someone who asked for less motion). It also closes the recipe form, as before.
- **The scrolls that are on purpose are not touched,** because they do not change the page: Recipes' Cookbook / Imported buttons and the import landing on Imported, Flyers' Jump to and back-to-top, Help's jump links, Inventory scrolling to a new shelf, the Planner's Recipe / Replace scrolling to the search and "Plan around this" scrolling to the Main meal banner. **A link that opens a page at a given spot** (a deal on Flyers, Plan around this, a slot on the Planner) carries where to land in `App.jsx`'s state and the page scrolls there itself once it is drawn (a smooth `scrollIntoView` in `requestAnimationFrame`), which happens after the reset, so it wins.
- **A page must not scroll the page by accident when it draws.** Flyers used to, as it appeared: it kept the highlighted category chip visible with `scrollIntoView`, which also scrolled the page down to its jump bar. It now scrolls only the row of chips (`FlyerDeals.jsx`). Do the same anywhere a small row has to follow a highlight: set the row's own `scrollLeft`.

Browser test: `e2e/page-top.spec.js` (every page from a page scrolled far down, on a computer and at 390px; Home's first screen; tapping the current tab; Plan around this and Flyers' back-to-top still scroll).

## Pictures in held or dragged things

One rule in `index.css` (`.press-hold :is(img, .picture-stand-in) { pointer-events: none }`), not one per page. A picture can be replaced under a finger: one that will not load, or loads late, becomes a placeholder (Inventory's `ItemPhoto`, the recipe form's `.re-photo-broken`). A touch whose element has left the page is lost: the browser takes it for a page scroll and the press-and-hold or drag is cancelled. With every picture inside the piece out of the way, the touch always lands on the piece itself, which stays; a tap on the picture still opens the piece.

- **Who carries `press-hold`:** Inventory's items when they can drag (`InventoryItem`), a Planner card (`PlannerMealCard`, on a computer and on a phone), a finder result and a Leftovers result when they can drag (`ResultCard`, `LeftoverResultCard`), and the recipe form's photo tiles (`SortablePhoto`). Grocery's rows and Store mode's rows hold no picture, and Makeable's cards and the recipe card are tapped, never held or dragged.
- **A new piece you can hold or drag** that shows a picture adds `press-hold` to its outer element. A placeholder that stands in for a picture (anything but an `<img>`) adds `picture-stand-in`. Do not write a `pointer-events: none` for one page's photo.
- **Today only Inventory (and the recipe form's tiles) take a picture out of the page when it fails;** `RecipePhoto` only hides it, so the Planner and the finder keep their `<img>`. The rule is what keeps that true if it ever changes.
- **The drag itself** is the app's one press-and-hold setup (`hooks/useHeldDrag.js`, see Held drag above). A piece that is held or dragged has `touch-action: pan-y` with the callout and text selection off, and its `DndContext` uses `HELD_DRAG` for its one sensor and `useDragTouchGuard` for the page. The recipe form's photo tiles used to have a second, touch sensor and `touch-action: manipulation`: on a phone the pointer sensor took the touch first, never waited for the hold, and the browser took it for a page scroll.
- **Tests:** `e2e/picture-fails.js` holds a picture's request until the test lets it fail, so it fails while the finger is down (during the hold, or while the piece is carried). `e2e/inventory-compact-tiles.spec.js` (Inventory, fails without the rule) and `e2e/planner-mobile.spec.js` (a Planner card) use it.

## Home's two screens

Home (`components/Home.jsx`; design `docs/design/riso-v2-home-two-screens/`, README first) is **one scroller holding two screens**, with a column of dots on the right edge. Only the layout is the design's; every card is the one it was. **Screen 1** is the greeting, the hero beside the Grocery card, and the week strip. **Screen 2** is Proteins on sale, Makeable now (only when it has something) and Use it up, in the same grid as before (three across, or two when Makeable steps aside, one column on a phone).

- **The shell.** `App.jsx` adds `is-home` to `.app` while the Home tab shows, and only then. That makes `.app` exactly `100dvh` tall (a flex column, `overflow: hidden`): the header keeps its own height and `.home-page` takes the rest (`flex: 1 1 0; min-height: 0`). **No header height is written down**: it is 84 px on a computer, 140 px when the tabs wrap onto a second row (about 1000 px wide) and 108 px on a phone, and it moves with the language and the fonts. So the page never scrolls on Home and every other page keeps its own shell. The design's header has no gap below it, so on Home the header's bottom margin is 0 and the screens' top padding (24 px, 18 on a phone) is the gap.
- **The scroller** (`.home-scroller`): `overflow-y: auto`, `position: relative` (the screens' `offsetTop` counts from it), `scroll-behavior: smooth`, no scrollbar. **One scroller runs through both screens**: a screen taller than the window is not a scroller of its own. A screen is `min-height: 100%` of it (not the README's `container-type: size` and `100cqh`: the same result, with no container query and nothing that could shut the Proteins card in under its scrim, which is on `<body>` at z-index 60; the open card is z-index 61). Side padding is on the screens (40 px, 18 on a phone), so the scroller is full width and the dots sit at the edge.
- **How it moves: `scrollMode`** (`lib/homeScreens.js`, unit-tested). **A computer scrolls freely at every size**, with the mouse wheel and the trackpad, from anywhere on the page (no snap, no flick stop). **A touch screen** (`TOUCH_SCREEN`, `(pointer: coarse)`, followed by `useMediaQuery`) **snaps where both screens fit**, a tablet: `screensFit` is true when both screens are no taller than the scroller (Home measures it with a `ResizeObserver`; the sizes move with the window and with a card's data) and Home sets `data-snap="on"`, which turns on `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory`. **A phone** (a touch screen where they don't fit) scrolls freely with the flick stop (`data-flick-stop="on"`, below). Why a computer never snaps (Matt's test of Oct 13, reproduced in Chromium at 1280 × 720, 1440 × 900, 1920 × 1080 and 1000 × 800): with the snap, a trackpad scroll shorter than half a screen springs back and a slow one wobbles hundreds of times without leaving screen 1 (the test tool's wheel events reach Chrome as a trackpad's pixel steps; a real mouse notch in Chrome may jump a whole screen, which could not be checked). And in a screen taller than the window a snap point is a wall: ordinary swipes and wheel ticks bounce back from it, and only a drag of more than half a screen or a flick gets across, so a phone doesn't snap either.
- **The flick stop, on a phone.** **To turn it off, set `HOME_FLICK_STOP` to `false` in `lib/homeScreens.js`: that one line brings back free scrolling on a phone, and the browser tests that need it skip themselves.** With it on (Matt's iPhone, Oct 13: a flick from screen 1 flew past the start of screen 2), a flick never carries the scroller across the join between the screens. Going down, a flick that set off above the top of screen 2 stops with that top at the top of the scroller; going up, one that set off below the end of screen 1 stops with that end at the bottom of the scroller (so from deep in screen 2 it runs past the top of screen 2 and stops at the join). Inside a screen it runs free, a flick that runs out before the join rests where it stops (nothing pulls it), and a finger on the screen is never stopped, so dragging across the join and reading stay free: no wall. The dots still jump to the top of each screen. The rule is `flickStop` (`lib/homeScreens.js`, unit-tested: where a coast stops, from where it began, where it is and its last step, stopping one step early rather than one late); `useHomeFlickStop` (`hooks/`) watches the scroller while `data-flick-stop="on"` (`scrollMode` "flick-stop": a phone, with the switch on). A coast is what follows a lifted finger after a swipe (a tap doesn't count), until 200 ms pass with no scroll. A stop sets the scroller on the join and holds it there until the coasting is over, putting back only movement past the join in the coast's own direction: it turns scrolling off (`overflow-y: hidden`, which ends the coasting) until the scroller has been still for 100 ms; on a slow device the coasting can run on for a few frames before that takes hold, and what it moves past the join is put back (tested with the processor slowed four times). **It only ever catches a coast, never a scroll the person or the app asks for:** a finger or the mouse pressed anywhere, a key (not Shift, Ctrl, Alt or Cmd alone) or the focus moving ends the coast and the hold at once (so a dot, a link or a card, and a `scrollIntoView` after a tap go where they're asked), and anything that moves the scroller other than the coast itself ends the hold and stays where it went. **It listens to touch only. The wheel and trackpad part (PR #156: a stop at the join for the wheel, held while it kept turning, with the wheel events cancelled and put back) and its tests were removed, not switched off**: on Matt's computer (Oct 13) it made the trackpad janky and Home stopped at the join for as long as you kept scrolling, so the top of screen 1 and the end of screen 2 couldn't be reached in one go. No wheel listener is left on Home. Tested in Chromium with real touch flicks at 390 × 664 and 390 × 844 in French with all three cards on screen 2. The test checks the rule, not glide distances: a long or fast flick that would cross the join stops exactly on it (started 400 to 740 px from the join, which it would otherwise overshoot by 2.5 times or more), a gentle one that runs out first rests where it stops (started at least 550 px away; it glides about 220), and inside a screen it runs free. The finger is in the screens' left margin, where only Home scrolls, and its touch events carry exact timestamps, so the speed the browser reads is the same on a busy machine. Safari (WebKit) could not be tested: its download is blocked in the build machine, Playwright can't send touch swipes to WebKit, and WebKit on Linux doesn't coast like an iPhone; turning `overflow` off is the known way to stop iOS momentum, but it is checked on Matt's iPhone, not in a test.
- **The dots** (`nav.home-dots`, named « Écrans de l'accueil » / "Home screens"): a button for each screen, `aria-label` "Screen 1" / « Écran 1 » (`home.screen` with `{n}`) and `aria-current="true"` on the lit one. A hit area 24 px wide, 34 px tall for the current screen and 20 otherwise, with a 10 px pill (26 px when current, the ink fill; the paper fill otherwise). `activeScreen` lights the second once half the scroller's height has passed its top (`scrollTop + clientHeight / 2 >= screen2.offsetTop`, recalculated on scroll, one frame at a time). A tap scrolls to that screen. The dots are centred on the area under the header (`top: 50%` of `.home-page`, which starts under it; the README's `calc(50% + 40px)` was for a box that included the header), `z-index: 40`, under the Proteins scrim.
- **The dots never cover a card.** They live in the gutter beside the cards. On a computer the cards stop 40 px from the edge and the dots begin 34 px from it (24 px wide, 10 px from the edge). On a phone the cards keep the design's 18 px on both sides and the dots' tap area is **18 px wide, flush with the edge** (the README's 24 px at 2 px would run 8 px over the cards), so no card is under a dot and a tap on a card's edge never hits one. More side padding was tried and is not the fix: at 390 px in French it wraps « Protéines en rabais » and « Circulaires → » and clips the product counts. The pill (10 px) can run a few pixels over the 8 px hard shadow of the hero and Grocery cards (not tappable); a 2 px paper ring around each dot keeps it apart from that shadow, and is invisible on the page. A browser test checks that no card reaches the dots at the top, the middle, the join and the end, that a tap just left of the dots is not a dot, and that the phone's cards are still 354 px wide.
- **Reduced motion:** `scroll-behavior: auto` and no dot transition (the app's global rule already stops transitions; this block also stops the smooth scroll). **Home always opens on screen 1:** Home is made new each time the tab opens, so the scroller starts at the top and the first dot is lit.
- **Unchanged:** the week strip still slides sideways on a phone and centres on today (only the strip moves); the Proteins pop-out still opens above everything. **Use it up's list** scrolls inside its card; on a phone it keeps a swipe to itself (`overscroll-behavior: contain`), and **on a computer** (`(pointer: fine)`) it hands the wheel and the trackpad on to Home once it reaches its end, so Home scrolls with the pointer anywhere on the page (before, the wheel did nothing over the list at its end).

Tests: `lib/homeScreens.test.js`; `e2e/home-two-screens.spec.js`: on a computer at 1280 × 720, 1440 × 900, 1920 × 1080 and 1000 × 800, the wheel and the trackpad (pixel steps) move Home with the pointer over each part of the page, never pulled back, all the way to both ends; **must pass: with the pointer over Proteins on sale, both ways**; Use it up's list hands the wheel on at its end; a tablet snaps; a phone's flicks stop at the join. Screenshots: `docs/screenshots/riso-v2-home-two-screens/`.

## Makeable now rule

"Makeable now" counts **meals only**, and **"Meals" means the same thing everywhere** (Recipes' Meals chip, Makeable, Home, the Planner's Makeable now): a recipe with **breakfast, lunch or supper among its meal types that is not « plat préparé »** (`isMealRecipe`). A side that is also a supper is a meal; a croissant recipe (breakfast AND prepared) is not. Recipes with no type yet count as meals for Makeable (nothing disappears before it is sorted). Sides, snacks, desserts, pantry / prep recipes (sauces live there) and prepared recipes are left out, unless "Include sides, desserts and prepared" is on.

- `isMakeableMeal(recipe, includeSides)` and `makeableRuleOn(includeSides, narrowedTo)` in `lib/mealSlots.js`. When someone narrows to a left-out kind themselves (the Meal menu on Sides, Dessert or Prepared), the rule does not apply.
- `useIncludeSides()` is the one saved setting (this browser), so every page agrees.
- `IncludeSidesToggle` (`components/RisoControls.jsx`) is the switch, « Inclure accompagnements, desserts et plats préparés » / "Include sides, desserts and prepared".
- What is missing comes from the one matcher (`recipeHave` in `lib/inventoryMatch.js`, see Ingredient-to-Inventory matching); Home's card is `makeableNow` in `lib/homeWeek.js`.
- **« Masquer de Faisable » / "Hide from Makeable"** is Matt's own choice for one recipe (avocado toast: two ingredients, always "ready"), the add-only `Recipe.hiddenFromMakeable` column (`20261022120000_recipe_hide_from_makeable`, off for every recipe), read in ONE place: `isHiddenFromMakeable` in `lib/mealSlots.js`. `isMakeableMeal` is `!isHiddenFromMakeable && isMakeableKind`, so Recipes' "makeable now" count and Home's card follow with no other change, **whatever "Include sides, desserts and prepared" says** (that setting is about kinds of recipes and keeps its meaning). `findRecipes` (`lib/finder.js`) is the one trap: it skips the kind rule when the rule is off (`ruleOn`), so it asks `isHiddenFromMakeable` itself for "Ready now" and "One or two short" on the page, and the counts. **Hidden is not a meal type:** `isMealRecipe`, `hasSlot` and `recipeSlots` ignore it, so the recipe stays on Recipes, under its meal types and in the Planner's search (only the Planner's "Ready now" chip and its count leave it). **A hidden recipe planned this week stays in Meals of the week** (`findRecipes` gets `plannedThisWeek`, the page's `plannedDays`): hiding stops Makeable from suggesting it, a planned meal is still the week; it is still out of Ready now and the other chips' counts. Tonight's card on Home (`makeableNow().results`) is not a suggestion and still shows it. Set from the recipe card's ⋯ menu and the editor's "Hide from Makeable" switch; Makeable lists the hidden ones under the cards in the shared `RemovedStrip` (`Makeable.jsx`), closed to begin with, with « Afficher de nouveau » (`onShowAgain`, the same `handleSetMakeableHidden`, Toast with Undo). `lib/mealSlots.test.js`, `lib/finder.test.js` and `e2e/makeable-hide.spec.js` guard it.

Used by: Recipes (the "makeable now" count in the line at the top, with the toggle beside it: **the toggle only changes that count, never the list**, so sides and desserts are never hidden from the cookbook), Home (Makeable now card), Makeable (every section and count, with its "Include sides, desserts and prepared" switch at the end of the count line), the finder (Ready count and results).

## Confirm dialog

`ConfirmDialog` is the app's own "are you sure?" question, in the Riso v2 style instead of the browser's `window.confirm`: a small centred pop-up (white card, black outline, hard shadow) over a dimmed page, with a question and two pill buttons. ## Recipe store

`lib/recipeStore.js` is where every recipe lives, the way `lib/dealsStore.js` holds the flyer deals. `App.jsx` loads the list once (`loadRecipes`), and everything that changes a recipe goes through the store: `addRecipe` (a new or imported recipe, first in the list), `saveRecipe` (what the server sent back after Edit recipe, notes, tags, Cookbook / Imported; it replaces the recipe whole, and a recipe the store no longer has is not brought back), `hideRecipe` / `showRecipe` (Delete and its Undo) and `clearRecipes` on logout. `useRecipeStore()` gives `{ recipes, byId, hidden }` and re-renders on any change. No page keeps its own list of recipes.

**Planned meals read their recipe through the store.** Each meal the server sends (`GET /api/planner`, `/api/planner/upcoming`) has a copy of its recipe joined in, as old as that read. `liveEntries` (`lib/liveRecipes.js`, with tests) swaps that copy for the store's recipe by id and leaves out the meals of a hidden recipe; the copy stays only for a recipe the store has not got (a note's placeholder made after the list loaded). `useLiveEntries(rows)` is the hook. `App.jsx` keeps the rows as the server sent them (`plannerRows`, `upcomingRows`) and passes the live ones down as `plannerEntries` and `upcomingEntries`. Home (its three lists), Grocery (its meals, the copy kept from the last visit included), the slot picker's other weeks and the Planner's last-week count read their own meals the same way.

- **A page that reads meals from the server uses `useLiveEntries`.** `lib/recipeStoreGuard.test.js` fails when a file reads `listPlanner` or `listPlannerUpcoming` without it, when the recipes list is read anywhere but `App.jsx`, or when a page keeps its own `[recipes, setRecipes]`.
- **A handler that changes the meals in state matches them by id** (`without` in `App.jsx`): the live meals it reads are not the same objects as the rows in state. A meal that needs no change keeps its identity, so what depends on the list does not run again.
- **Delete and Undo.** Delete hides the recipe's id: the recipe and its meals leave every page at once. Undo shows it again, where it was. The delete is sent when the toast is gone (`showToast`'s `onGone`); if it fails, the recipe is shown again and the toast says so. After a delete the id stays hidden, so a page that still holds an older read of the Planner cannot bring its meals back.
- **Grocery's tidy-ups go by the server's meals.** Grocery clears a saved removal, own amount or check once no planned meal needs the item (`staleOverrideKeys`, `tidyChecks`). They read the rows as the server sent them (`entryRows` in `GroceryList.jsx`), not the live ones, so a recipe deleted a moment ago, which Undo can still bring back, does not lose its saved checks.
- **Pending saves** (below) are unchanged: a read of grocery data still waits for a recipe edit or delete in flight. The store only lays the current recipe over whatever meals the server returned.

## Pending saves

A change shows on screen at once and is saved in the background. A page opened right after (the Grocery tab, Store mode, the recipe card's ✓) used to ask the server for the data before that save had landed, and showed the old one. So `request` in `api.js` keeps every save that can change the grocery list in `lib/pendingSaves.js` until it ends, and **a read of grocery data or of upcoming meals first waits for those saves, and is read again (at most twice) if a save started or ended while it was on its way**, because that answer can be older than the change. This is built once, in the data code every page uses: a page reads through `api` as it always did and does nothing extra. Do not wait for saves in a page.

There is a second area, **`links`** (`LINKS`): the names Matt taught the matcher (`/inventory-links`). A read of them waits for the teach and remove saves already sent, and nothing else waits for them (the matcher reads the list that is in memory, which a change updates at once). A third, **`names`** (`NAMES`), is Matt's changes to the ingredient names (`/ingredient-names` to read, `/admin/ingredient-names` to save): the same rule.

What the grocery list is made of, which is what counts (`saveArea` and `readArea` in `lib/pendingSaves.js` say which request is which, with tests):

- **Saves:** every `/grocery…` change; **every Planner save** (`/planner…`: placing, moving, removing, noting, copying, marking a meal; the grocery list is built from the planned meals, so a read right after planning a meal must see it); and a recipe **edited or deleted by its id** (`PUT` or `DELETE /recipes/:id`: its ingredients are on the list through the meals that use it). A recipe link being read (`/recipes/scrape`) or a pasted list (`/recipes/parse-ingredients`) saves nothing and can be slow, so it is not one.
- **Reads that wait:** every `/grocery…` read and `GET /planner/upcoming` (`api.listPlannerUpcoming`, which Grocery, Home and `App.jsx` read the meals from). The Planner's own week (`GET /planner?week=`) does not wait.

One more piece is for a page that loads several things together and then puts them on screen (the Grocery list): `savesVersion("grocery")` changes when a save starts and when it ends. If it changed while the page was loading, the page loads again (`GroceryList.jsx`, at most twice more) instead of putting the older answers over the person's change.

**Saves in order.** Quick taps on one item used to send their saves together, and the server could finish them in another order (the box said queued, a reload said bought). `saveInOrder({ area })` (`lib/saveInOrder.js`, with tests) queues each item's saves one after another; `run(key, save)` resolves with `{ ok, latest }`, so only the newest tap undoes its change when its save fails, and with `area` the whole queue counts as a save in progress here from the moment of the tap, so a read waits for saves that haven't started yet. The grocery checkbox (`setStep` in `GroceryList.jsx`) uses it; a browser test slows the first save and checks the last tap wins.

Used by: every `api.listGrocery…` read, every grocery save (`addGroceryExtra`, `deleteGroceryExtra`, `setGroceryOverride`, the checks (one item's in order, above), the stores), every Planner save (`placeOnPlanner`, `removeFromPlanner`, `updatePlannerEntry`, `markSlotBlank`, `setPlannerEntryNote`, `copyPlannerWeek`), `updateRecipe` and `deleteRecipe`, including Home's Proteins on sale (its Add to list, and Home's grocery card reading again after it).

## Quebec day

A date the app shows or counts is a day in Quebec, not a moment on the device's clock or the server's (UTC): at 11 p.m. in Quebec it is already the next day in UTC. `lib/quebecDay.js` is the one rule (`America/Toronto`); `server/src/lib/quebecDay.js` is the same code for the server (the AI limit's "today", the leftover keep-time bar), and `server/src/lib/quebecDay.test.js` checks the two agree. Do not count days with `Date.now()` or the device's date.

- `quebecDay(moment)` is the Quebec day ("2026-10-12"); `addDays`, `daysBetween` do the arithmetic on days.
- **A use-by date is a day** (`expiryDay`). A day typed or picked is sent as "2026-10-16" and the database keeps it as midnight UTC, and that is the day it says (so dates already saved read right). Any other moment (a USDA shelf life worked out from a time) is the Quebec day it falls on. Never cut a saved date with `slice(0, 10)`: that is the UTC day.
- **Days left** is `daysUntil` (`lib/pantryInventory.js`): Quebec calendar days from today, 0 on the use-by day itself, the same at 9 a.m. and 11 p.m. Inventory, Home, Makeable, the finder, the recipe card, Flyers (`daysLeft` for a flyer's end day) and Grocery's sale days (and which flyer deals are still running there) all count it. **The use-by day itself is not expired:** the count is 0 and everything says today (Inventory's card "expires today" / « expire aujourd'hui » from `expiry.today`, the add form's tag "Today" / « Aujourd'hui » from `days.today`, Home's Use it up "today!" / « aujourd'hui! », the finder's leftover card and Cook with chip, the recipe card's "Expires today"), the item keeps a short pink stub of life left (`lineForDays`) and is counted under "to use soon", and Home still lists it. **Expired is only below 0**, the day after: the Expired tag and pink-tinted card, the form's Expired tag and note, and the expired count. The Planner's "past fridge life" sticker (`leftoverIsStale`) says a leftover meal is late only when its day comes after the use-by day, so a meal on the use-by day is fine. Every rule that tells expired from not expired is `days < 0` (`expiryTag`, `lineForDays`, `useByKind` in `lib/inventoryForm.js`); do not write `days <= 0`.
- **Today in the Inventory form** is the Quebec day (`dateFromDays`, `daysFromDate`), so the use-by chips ("3 days", "1 week") land on the right day on any device.
- Leftovers: `leftoverExpiry` gives the use-by day for `fridgeLifeDays` or 4 days from the Quebec day it is put away.

  - **What is listed** (`lib/cookPrep.js`, unit tests in `cookPrep.test.js`, no AI): each ingredient whose `notes` say something to do to it (`prepNote`: "to taste", "optional", "divided", "for serving", "low sodium", "unsalted", "boneless", can sizes like "796 ml" and the French equivalents are not preparation and are left off; in a note that also says what to do they are dropped). Groups, in this order, with empty ones hidden: **Cut**, **Measure**, **Squeeze / zest**, **Other** (`prepGroup`: the first group whose word list, English and French, the note has a word from: squeeze, then cut, then measure, then other). The **how line** (`howLine`) is the note as an order: a leading "finely chopped" / "peeled and diced" / « finement haché » becomes "Finely chop" / "Peel and dice" / « Hachez finement » (« vous »); a shape (« en dés ») gets its verb (« Coupez en dés »); anything else stays as written with a capital ("cut into 3 cm pieces"). It stays in the note's language, which can differ from the app's, and never gets a size the recipe doesn't give. The **step tag** ("STEP 2", "STEPS 2 · 3") comes from `stepIngredients`; no match, no tag. **Do first** (`doFirstSentence`) is the recipe's own first sentence that says preheat / « préchauffer » / heat the oven, in the steps' order, with the other unit added after each temperature (`withBothTemperatures`: °C to the nearest 10, °F to the nearest 25, so 425 °F is 220 °C); no such sentence, no card. Step 1 is never merged or hidden, even when it is just "Prep".
  - **Ticks** are the ones in `checked`, keyed `${stepIndex}:${name}` as "For this step" has them. Tapping a prep row sets the tick in every step that uses the ingredient, and the row shows ticked when all of them are; an ingredient no step uses has its own key (`prep:${name}`). A tick is never required to continue.
  - **Drawn like a step:** the rows are `.cm-uses-row` rows (the same check, amount column and ticked look) with `.cm-prep-*` for what is added: group headers, the how line and the step tag (at the right on a computer, on its own line under the how line on a phone). The amount column is one grid for all groups so the names line up, and grows to the longest amount. The middle column is the same scrolling `.cm-left` as a step (the fitting rules, which shrink one step's text, do not apply: a long list just scrolls, on a computer too, with the bars in place). Do first is under the photo on a computer and above the groups on a phone, which has no photo.
- **Last step:** Next says I cooked this (`cooked.button`, « Je l'ai cuisiné ») and Up next is hidden; it opens the finished view over Cook mode (`onFinish`, through the recipe card to `openCooked` in `App.jsx`). Cook mode stays under it: "Back to step 1" there comes back to step 1, × and "Back to the app" close both.
- **Closing:** × or Escape closes it at once. The step timers belong to the recipe card (`useStepTimers` lives in `RecipeDetailModal`), so a timer carries on there and in Cook mode when it is reopened; nothing is asked.
- **Which ingredients a step uses** (`stepIngredients`, matching in `lib/ingredientMatch.js`): a name matches when one of its words is a whole word of the step. Short names count ("egg", "oil", « ail », « riz »), and a word inside another word does not ("rice" is not in "price", "oil" is not in "boil"). In a longer name a word of three letters or fewer counts only when it is a known food (`SHORT_FOODS`, or any word in the French / English pairs), so the "dry" of "dry white wine" and the "all" of "all-purpose flour" never match a step that merely says "dry" or "all". Singular and plural match either way ("tomatoes" / "tomato", « oignon » / « oignons »), with the grocery list's singular rules (`singularize`) and accents ignored. The other language counts: the one-word French / English pairs the flyers use (`PHRASES` in `lib/bilingual.js`: « ail » is garlic, « riz » is rice) and whole phrases through `frenchToEnglish` (« pommes de terre » are potatoes). A colour, a size, "fresh", "ground" or a little word (« de », "the") never matches on its own, so "red onion" is found by "onion" and not by "red pepper"; « thé » / "the" and « maïs » / « mais » (but) are kept apart. One matching, so a change to it changes "For this step" and the prep page's step tags together.
- **Also:** ← → (and a swipe on a touch screen) move between steps, Space starts or pauses the timer, Keep screen on holds a wake lock, amounts and the numbers in the text scale with the servings.

## Ingredient-to-Inventory matching

`lib/inventoryMatch.js` is **the one matcher in the app**: it says which Inventory item is a recipe ingredient, for every page that decides "have it or not" and for the finished view's take-out list (`buildTakeOut`). Do not compare Inventory with a recipe any other way (`core()` in `lib/similarRecipes.js` is only for comparing recipes with each other, the grocery list and flyer deals). Two names are the same food once both are read in English: French names count (« Poulet », « Lait 2 % », « ail », « banane », « pommes de terre »): they are read from the table of ingredient names with Matt's changes on top (`lib/frenchReading.js`: every French spelling of a row, singular and plural, accents folded, is read as the row's first English name, and the matcher reads it again whenever the list changes), plus the matcher's own few words (`lib/matcherWords.js`: « haché », « bouillon », « zeste », the cuts of a meat, tap water). `lib/bilingual.js` (the flyers' word pairs) is no longer read by the matcher, food words in any order, prep and descriptor words left out ("whole", "2%", "unsalted", "boneless"); a colour or variety ("yellow", "basmati", "greek") and a cut ("thighs") only separate two foods when both names give one; a stock, powder, paste, sauce or juice is never the food it is made of; a meal (a leftover, or a prepared meal the person tagged) never matches (`isMealItem`). Salt, oil and spices are ordinary ingredients, had when they are in Inventory, and French names count (« sel », « poivre », « huile végétale », « farine », « cassonade », « poudre à pâte », « bicarbonate de soude », « fécule de maïs », « origan »...); plain pepper is the spice while a bell pepper or a coloured pepper (« poivrons », "red pepper") is the vegetable, and the two never match; several foods on one line ("salt and pepper", « sel et poivre, au goût ») are looked up one by one (`foodsOnLine`) and the line is had when each is; water from the tap (`isTapWater`: "cold water", « eau froide ») is never to buy and is not a line. Several items for one ingredient: the one still good with the soonest use-by first, the rest from the next. `buildTakeOut` scales the recipe's amounts to the servings cooked, adds up an ingredient listed twice, converts to the item's unit (weights with weights, volumes with volumes, counts with counts, `convertAmount`) and asks when it can't. Unit tests: `lib/inventoryMatch.test.js`.

More names are one food, in both languages (the table of expected answers is `lib/inventoryMatchRules.test.js`, YES and must-stay-NO): describing words are left out on both sides ("low sodium", "reduced sodium", "low fat", "reduced fat", "fat free", "no salt added", "full fat", "liquid", "unwaxed", "bulb", and their Quebec French forms, feminine ones too: `DESCRIBING`), but not "dark" (dark soy sauce is another sauce) and not "seasoned" (seasoned salt is not salt); a style word that names its own product (`STYLE_WORDS`: balsamic, dijon, sriracha, hoisin, worcestershire, tabasco, teriyaki, tahini, miso, gochujang, harissa) is that product with or without it, while plain vinegar still does not cover balsamic; a few names are one food whatever the word order or language (`SYNONYMS`: rice wine vinegar = rice vinegar, apple cider vinegar = cider vinegar, red vinegar = red wine vinegar; wine vinegar covers red and white); one name covers another one way only (`ALSO_COVERS`: seasoned rice vinegar covers rice vinegar); a whole lime or lemon covers its juice and zest and an orange its zest, never the other way (`FRUIT_PARTS`); an egg (« œuf ») covers a recipe's "egg yolk", "egg white", « jaune d'œuf » and « blanc d'œuf », never the other way (`FOOD_PARTS`: yolks and whites in Inventory do not make a recipe's eggs had); an orange pepper is a bell pepper (« poivron orange »); a sausage, bacon, ham, chorizo, prosciutto or pancetta is a food of its own (`MADE_MEATS`), made of pork unless the name says another animal ("turkey bacon" is not bacon), and never a pork chop; a liver, kidney, fat, rind, nugget, salad, soup, curry, gravy, bowl, pie, burger, meatball, sandwich, mushroom, tomato or "corned" next to an animal makes another food (`NOT_THE_ANIMAL`: "duck fat", "shrimp salad", "beef tomatoes" and "oyster mushrooms" are not duck, shrimp, beef or oysters); a synonym is swapped word by word (`synonymOf` in `lib/ingredientFamilies.js`: "chilli" is "chile"), so a chilli jam, chilli flakes or a chili con carne are not the chile pepper; a roma tomato is a plum tomato, not a cherry, grape or vine one (`VARIETY_KINDS`); goat cheese is not a goat; « mûre » / « mûres » is blackberries on its own or after « de », « aux » or a number, and "ripe" after another word (« cerises mûres »; `also` in `lib/frenchReading.js` holds the word's other meaning); a green or red bean (« haricot vert », « haricot rouge ») is its own food and does not cover plain beans, as in English; coriander on its own is cilantro (ground coriander and coriander seeds stay the spice, and bare coriander still covers ground coriander); and lime, banana, grape, vine and curry leaves are products of their own (`OWN_LEAF_RE`), while basil, mint and bay leaves are their herb. **The wide check** (`lib/inventoryMatchWide.test.js`, with `lib/fixtures/` and `scripts/inventoryMatchWide.mjs`) asks the matcher about every one of a few thousand real names (TheMealDB's ingredients, FoodKeeper's products, every spelling of the names table in both languages, the matcher's own French words, the names in the tests) against every other, and fails if a match it gave before is lost; a wrong match that went away on purpose is listed in the test with its reason. When the matcher began to read its French from the table (4,301 names; the numbers and the grouped lists of what went and what came are in the pull request, #179), 1,910 matches were gained and 5,471 went away, all on purpose. The gains are the two languages agreeing (a French spelling and its English, singular and plural, « gousse d'ail » / "garlic clove", « banane », « petits pois ») and French names catching up with rules English already had (garlic and a clove of it, tomatoes and a cherry tomato). The losses are wrong matches: the singular « pilon de poulet » and « côtelette de porc » used to match every cut of the animal, « citrons verts » was a lemon, a yolk or a white was an egg, « haricot vert » was a plain bean, and the English rule that any name with an animal in it was the animal (so a sausage, bacon or ham was a pork chop, and « canard » was duck fat, « crevette » shrimp salad and « tomate cerise » a roma tomato) would have spread to every French name the table gave the matcher.

### Taught names

« Je l'ai comme… » / "I have it as…". When a recipe names something Inventory keeps under another name that the rules do not know, Matt teaches the matcher the pair. The button is **I have it as…** in a To-buy row's drawer on the recipe card (next to I have it), a small ≈ beside each To-buy chip on the planned-meal card, and a link on the meta line of a Grocery row that comes from recipes. All three call `ask(names, { label })` from `useInventoryLinks()` (`InventoryLinksContext`, provided by `App.jsx`), which opens the shared `Popup` (the bottom sheet on a phone): a search box over Inventory items, never a leftover or a prepared meal. One pick saves at once, with the toast "Rice wine vinegar now counts as Rice vinegar" and Undo. **It adds nothing to Inventory**, so it does not go through the confirmation sheet and `CLAUDE.md`'s rule is untouched; "I have it" is the one that adds, through the sheet. Help for Grocery and Inventory tells the two apart.

- **Admin only, for now** (HQ task jlc2 reworks it for everyone, after the beta). The rule is the one the Admin page uses, never a second check: the server reads `ADMIN_EMAILS` (`checkAdmin` and `requireAdmin` in `server/src/lib/admin.js`) and the client reads `user.isAdmin`, the flag that shows the Admin menu. For anyone else: `App.jsx` provides `InventoryLinksContext` as `null`, so the To-buy drawer button, the planned-meal chip's ≈, the Grocery row link, "Also counts as" in the Inventory item pop-up and "Counts because you said…" in the recipe drawer are all gone (each already shows nothing when the context is empty; a new place that offers it must do the same); `POST` and `DELETE /api/inventory-links` answer 403 (`requireAdmin`) and `GET` answers an empty list; and the Help text about it is left out. The matcher is unchanged: it reads whatever links the app holds, which for a tester is none.
- **Help text for admins only**: a line, or part of one, written between `<admin>` and `</admin>` in `en.js` / `fr.js` is shown to an admin and left out for everyone else (`helpTextFor` and `helpLinesFor` in `lib/helpCopies.js`, used by `Help.jsx`, which `App.jsx` gives `isAdmin`). A line that is only admin text is dropped for a tester; lines keep their place, so English and French still line up one for one. `i18n.test.js` fails if a tester's Help names « Je l'ai comme… », its three button copies or "Also counts as", or if the two languages differ in which parts are admin-only. Giving it back to everyone is removing the markers and the `null` in `App.jsx`.

- **What is stored** (table `InventoryLink`, `GET/POST/DELETE /api/inventory-links`, signed in only, and for now admins only: 403 on POST and DELETE, an empty list on GET for anyone else): the recipe-side name, the Inventory-side name (folded keys, `linkKey`) and the two as Matt saw them (labels). Names, not item ids or matcher keys, so a new bottle with the same name still counts and a later change to the rules does not break a link. Unique per account and pair; POST is idempotent, refuses an empty or identical pair, and stops at 300 links; DELETE is only for your own (404 otherwise). The server only keeps the pairs and does no matching.
- **How it is read**: `sameFoodAs` in `lib/inventoryMatch.js`, right after the matcher's own rules, and nowhere else (`lib/inventoryLinks.test.js` fails otherwise). A link applies to exactly those two names (red onion <- yellow onion does not leak to other onions), one way, one hop (it never chains into another link). A meal never covers anything, and an item past its date still does not count. The list is in memory (`lib/inventoryLinks.js`), so every page reads it; the matcher's kept answers start again when it changes, and `App.jsx` hands Inventory on as a new array so every page asks again.
- **How it is saved** (`lib/linkSaves.js`, tests beside it): the screen changes first, the save goes out behind the saves already made for the same pair (`saveInOrder` in the `links` pending-saves area), so teach, Undo, teach ends as the last tap says; a failed newest save puts the screen back and says so. A Grocery row that stands for several names (garlic and garlic cloves) teaches a link for each name, and one Undo removes them all (`foodsToTeach` picks, for each name, the food on its line that Inventory does not hold).
- **Seeing and removing**: the Inventory item pop-up lists "Also counts as: Tamari ×" (`linksFromItem`), and a blue row's drawer says "Counts because you said…" with Remove (`linkBehind`). Both show a toast with Undo.

Names and units come from `lib/ingredientNames.js` (`canonicalize`, `singularize`, `capitalize`, `canonicalUnit`; `lib/groceryList.js` re-exports them), the layer both the matcher and the grocery list stand on; the grocery list reads several foods on one line by the matcher's `foodsOnLine`, so its rows ("Salt", "Pepper") are the foods the matcher looks up.

What the pages use (`kitchen` is App's `{ inventory }`, built once in `App.jsx` and passed down):

- `ingredientHave(name, kitchen)`: "have" or "need" for one ingredient. The recipe card's ✓ beside each line.
- `recipeHave(recipe, kitchen)`: `{ have, buy, missing, totalCount, matchedCount, missingCount }`, one line per food in the recipe's order (two lines are one food when each covers the other: "garlic cloves" and "minced garlic"), named as the recipe writes it, water from the tap left out. The one count: the recipe card's receipt (`recipeLines`, the same lines, with `lineNeed` and `shortBy` for the amounts and what you'd run short of at other servings), the planned-meal card (`IngredientMarks`), the Planner's search and Makeable (through `rankRecipesForTray` in `lib/plannerSuggestions.js`, then `findRecipes` and `makeableSections`), Home's Makeable now and tonight's card (`makeableNow` in `lib/homeWeek.js`), the Recipes page's "makeable now" count and Fewest missing sort, and the recipe form's preview.
- `recipeLines(recipe, kitchen)`: the lines behind `recipeHave`, with each line's ingredients. The recipe card's receipt (`recipeReceipt` in `lib/recipeCard.js`) is built on it, with `lineNeed(ingredients, scale)` (one food's amounts added up in the first one's unit) and `shortBy(need, name, inventory)` (how much more to buy when your Inventory items, still good, hold too little; null when it can't be told, so it never guesses).
- `expiringItemsIn(recipe, inventory, plannerEntries, recipes, days)`: the "uses expiring" rule (the recipe card's "use soon", the Makeable card's pink strip, the Recipes count). The finder's "Expiring soon" and its ranking use `expiringSoon` and `recipeUsesItem`.
- Cook with: `recipeUsesItem(recipe, itemName)` finds the recipes using a pick (a pick of "Paprika" also finds "smoked paprika"), and `foodKey` lists each food once on the shelves; meals are not on the shelves.
- `recipesUsingItem(item, recipes)`: the item pop-up's "N of your recipes use X" (a meal has none). Home's "N recipes use these" under Use it up is `useUpRecipeCounts` in `lib/homeWeek.js`. Neither compares names as text any more.

**The guard.** `server/src/oneMealRule.test.js` (like `noPantryStaple.test.js`) reads the code and fails when a file writes its own "leftover or prepared meal" check, narrows a list of Inventory items by the leftover flag, reads an Inventory item's `isLeftover` / `isPreparedMeal` outside the short list of leftover-only files (each with its reason), compares recipe ingredient text with `includes(`, or brings back `coversIngredient`. It only knows those patterns: a new way of matching still has to go through the matcher.

The answers are kept per Inventory (a `WeakMap` on the array) until it or the day change. Unit tests: `lib/inventoryMatch.test.js`, and `lib/oneMatcher.test.js` checks one recipe gives the same answer on every page, in English and French; `e2e/one-matcher.spec.js` checks it in the app on a computer and at 390px in French.

## Ingredient names

What a recipe's ingredient is called in the language shown (FR | EN). **Display only**: the recipe keeps the words it was saved with (nothing here writes to the database), and everything that decides something (the Inventory matcher, the search, sale lookups, "add to list", what is saved) keeps using the name as written; the matcher and the search read the same table for their French and English, but they never change a name. A screen that draws a recipe's ingredient draws it through `ingredientName(name)` (or `useIngredientNames()` in a component, which also follows the FR | EN switch); a prep note ("finely chopped") through `ingredientNote(note, name)`. Never translate a name by hand in a component.

- **The table** is `lib/ingredientDisplay.txt`, one line per ingredient so it is easy to read and correct: `English singular | English plural | French singular | French plural | gender` (`m`, `f`, and `mp` / `fp` when the French is always plural: « épinards », « pâtes »). `=` in a plural column means the same as the singular, `/` gives other spellings that are understood (the first is shown), and a name is written the Quebec way (bleuets, sauce soya, poudre à pâte). Under it are the **describing words** (fresh, red, boneless: four French forms and where they go), the **prep words** (chopped, melted: four forms), the **prep adverbs** and the **fixed phrases** ("to taste"). A line is added only when the pair is not just the parts put together ("red onion" is "red" + "onion", « oignon rouge », so it has no line; "red pepper" is a bell pepper, so it has one).
- **How a name is read:** as a whole name first, then as a name with describing words around it, then part by part for "salt and pepper"; in either language (« oignons rouges » and "red onions" are one ingredient). A name already in the language asked for comes back exactly as it was. The number follows the written form (« oignons » for "onions"), the describing words and prep words agree with the name's gender and number (« poitrines de poulet désossées sans peau », « tomates hachées »), the case follows the written one, and a note is translated only when **every** word of it is in the table (one word that is not, and it is shown as written, so a note is never half translated). A written form that is a different ingredient in each language (« raisin » is a grape, English "raisin" is dried) is not read at all.
- **Where it is shown:** the recipe card (receipt, "uses" in a step, Similar chips), Cook mode (Before you start, with its how line made from the note in the language shown, and For this step), Makeable's pills, the Planner's meal card, the Finder's main-meal chips and "shares" lines, the "I have it as…" pop-up's title, the toast that says an ingredient came off the list, and the **Grocery rows built from recipes** (`label` on each row from `buildGroceryList`, read by Grocery, Store mode, "Already at home", the sale strip, the share text; the list is in A to Z order of the words shown). A French recipe read in English works the same way round.
- **What is never changed:** an Inventory item's name, a Grocery item Matt typed himself (`isManual`: its `label` is its name), the recipe editor, the recipe's steps and title, and Flyers (which read the deal's own French name). **What goes into Inventory is unchanged too**: the confirmation sheet proposes the recipe's own name, so a bought item does not depend on the screen's language.
- **Matt's changes (the overrides).** The Admin page's Ingredient names card lets Matt give a name its other language, correct any row, hide a wrong built-in row, and go back to the built-in row. They are stored in the add-only table `IngredientNameOverride` (migration `20261024120000_ingredient_names`): one row per change, with `replaces` (the built-in row it stands in for, by its first English singular folded, `row.key`; blank for an ingredient he added; one change per built-in row), `hidden`, the four names written the way the file writes a cell (`/` between other spellings, `=` for a plural that is the singular), `gender` (`m`, `f`, `mp`, `fp`) and who saved it last. They are translations of the app, not personal data, so they belong to nobody: `GET /api/ingredient-names` gives every signed-in account the same list (an admin also gets `updatedBy`, the email), and only an admin can `POST`, `PUT /:id` and `DELETE /:id` under `/api/admin/ingredient-names` (403 for anyone else). Removing a change deletes its row and the built-in row is back; the file is never changed.
- **One reader.** `setIngredientOverrides(list)` in `lib/ingredientList.js` (re-exported by `lib/ingredientDisplay.js`) rebuilds the lookup from the built-in rows with the changes applied (`effectiveRows` in `lib/ingredientTable.js`: Matt's rows first, so where a spelling is on two rows his is the one read; a hidden or replaced built-in row is left out; a change whose built-in row the file no longer has simply adds its names) and tells every screen (`useIngredientOverridesVersion`). `useIngredientNames()` follows it, and so does `AuthGate`, which already redraws the whole app on a language change, so a name drawn by a plain function (the Grocery rows' `label`, the "taken off the list" toast) follows too (`e2e/ingredient-name-edits.spec.js` loads the answer late and checks the Grocery list changes). `effectiveIngredientRows()` is the one merged list; the display, the Inventory matcher (`lib/frenchReading.js`) and the recipe search (`ingredientSearchTerms`) all read it, and follow `ingredientListVersion()`. The exports `ingredientRows`, `nounIndex`, `conflicts` and `ambiguous` stay the **built-in** file's own, which the table tests read.
- **How the app gets them.** `App.jsx` reads the list with its first data (not part of the "couldn't load" check: if it can't be read the built-in names are still right, so the app opens without them) and `lib/nameSaves.js` (`createNameSaver`) keeps it: a save, hide or remove shows at once, goes out behind the saves already made for that row (`saveInOrder`, the `names` area of `pendingSaves.js`), and puts the row back as the server has it, with a toast, if it fails. Each change gives back what was there before, which is what the toast's Undo restores. After every change (and when the list first arrives with something in it) `createNameSaver`'s `onChange` has `App.jsx` hand Inventory on as a new array, the way a taught name does, so every page asks the matcher again at once: the recipe card, Makeable, the Planner and Grocery change without a reload (`e2e/ingredient-matcher.spec.js`). A tester sees a change the next time they open or reload the app (nothing is pushed); on a slow connection the built-in word shows for a moment first.
- **What is allowed** (`cleanOverride`, `clashOf` in `lib/ingredientTable.js`, the same file in the form and on the server): an English and a French singular, a gender (masculine or feminine, plus "always plural"), a blank plural saved as `=`; a spelling at most 60 characters, a side at most 200 and 8 spellings, no `|`; at most 1,000 changes; and **no spelling another row already has in the same language**, accents, capitals, apostrophes and hyphens ignored (« raisin » as a different food in the other language is fine). The row being changed and the built-in row it replaces do not count. **A French spelling may not be one of the matcher's own words** (`lib/matcherWords.js`: « poudre », « zeste », « feuille »...: the matcher reads them its own way, so the row would never count; `ingredientMatcherWord`). The form says a clash or a length as soon as it is typed and the rest (a missing name or gender) when Save is pressed; the server refuses the same things with the same messages in both languages (`ingredientTaken`, `ingredientMatcherWord`, ...). **The form also tells Matt what the matcher makes of the names** (`matcherNotes` in `lib/ingredientNameForm.js`, notes that never stop a save): a French spelling it already reads as another food (« citron » is lemon; the row would make it lime), two names it would not take for one food, and a spelling it never reads (« sale » is an English word; « raisin » is a different ingredient in each language). Hiding a built-in row also stops the matcher from reading its French, and the form says so.
- **The server reads the built-in file too.** `server/src/lib/ingredientNames.js` reads `client/src/lib/ingredientDisplay.txt` **once, when the server starts**, with the same `parseTable` and row rules (`lib/ingredientTable.js` imports nothing of the app but the plain data of `lib/matcherWords.js`, so both run it; there is no second parser and no second copy of the file; Render deploys the whole repo), so a save can be refused when it uses a built-in spelling or one of the matcher's own words (`checkOverride`). `builtInIngredientRows()` is that list. The server does not match or search recipes (both are the browser's), so it needs nothing more. If the file can't be read the server says so in its log and refuses changes (503) until it restarts; the rest of the app is not affected.
- **The matcher reads the table and the changes.** `lib/ingredientDisplayMatcher.test.js` fails when a row, built-in or changed (it runs the same checks on the merged list with sample changes: one added, one replaced, one hidden, one with an extra spelling), is not the same food to the matcher in both languages, when a row's French looks like a different row's food, and for the describing words. The rows it still reads differently on purpose are listed with the reason (`READ_DIFFERENTLY`: « raisin » is a grape in French and a dried grape in English, « thé » is never read because "the" is English; `WORDS_READ_DIFFERENTLY`: « salé », because "sale" is English); a listed row must still disagree, so a line comes off when it starts to agree. `lib/ingredientOverrides.test.js` guards that an added row counts at once, a hidden row stops counting, a matcher that has already answered for an Inventory answers again, and the matcher's own words win over a name. `lib/ingredientDisplay.test.js` checks the table itself (no written name on two lines, four forms and a gender on every line, every form turns into the other language's) and the rules above. Only French feeds the matcher: a row's other English spellings (cilantro / coriander) are not read, because "ground coriander" must stay apart from cilantro.
- **Names with no line yet** show as written, and the admin's Ingredient names card lists them (see Admin page): Matt fills them in there, for everyone. To move a name into the built-in file for good (a developer's job), add its line to the table, run `npx vitest run client/src/lib/ingredientDisplay`, then remove his change; the matcher and the search read the new line by themselves. The **prep notes** (and describing words) with no translation are still added in the file only.
- **The recipe search** (`lib/recipeSearch.js` `matchesSearch`, for Recipes, Makeable and the Planner's finder; `matchesIngredientSearch` in `lib/finder.js` for Cook with) finds either language: `ingredientSearchTerms(term)` gives every way the typed name can be written (a name that is a whole spelling of a row gives all of the row's spellings, one with describing words gives its other-language reading in both numbers, a spelling that is a different ingredient in each language gives only itself), so « coriandre » finds "fresh cilantro" and "cilantro" finds « coriandre fraîche » (and a recipe with ground coriander: a search finds more, it never decides « J'en ai »). Capitals, accents, apostrophes and hyphens are ignored, a term is still looked for as written (half a word finds what it always did), and a comma still means "any of". Each recipe's text is folded once and kept (a `WeakMap`, read again when the recipe is replaced), and the last query's terms are kept until the list changes, so a keystroke on 10,000 recipes takes about 8 ms. `lib/recipeSearch.test.js`.
- **Not in this piece:** recipe steps, Cook mode's "For this step" (`lib/ingredientMatch.js`), the flyers and the ingredient photos, which still read `lib/bilingual.js` (the flyers' word pairs, also used by the server's Flipp and price code).

## Reorder sections

`components/ReorderSections.jsx` is **the one way to move a whole section**: a store on Grocery's By store and a shelf on Inventory. Do not build a second one; a new list of sections to reorder uses it too. A page gives each section `useReorderSection({ id, data })` (its `ref`, the `style` that slides or carries it, `lifted`, and its `grip`), draws the ⠿ with `ReorderGrip` (a span in Grocery's heading, a button on a shelf), and wraps the sections in `ReorderList` (`ids`, and a `strategy`). What a drop does is the page's own: Grocery's `handleDragEnd` saves the store order, Inventory's `useReorderDrop` saves the layout.

- **How it feels (the same on both pages).** A mouse picks the ⠿ up after 6px of movement; a finger needs no hold (`REORDER_MOUSE`, `REORDER_TOUCH`: the ⠿ is `touch-action: none`, so a touch that starts on it never scrolls the page and can only be moving the section). The section itself lifts and follows the pointer (`.reorder-lifted`: 90% opacity, shadow, above the others), the others slide out of the way (dnd-kit's 200 ms transition), and it lands in the place of the section it is dropped on, with no extra animation. The new order shows at once and is then saved (Grocery's `saveStoreOrder`; Inventory's `handleSaveInventoryLayout`, which puts the old layout back if the save fails). **Item rows keep their own press-and-hold on both pages.** The page scrolls near the top and bottom of the screen (dnd-kit's auto-scroll).
- **Sections of different widths** (Inventory's shelves: Full, Half, Third on a 12-column grid). `gridSortingStrategy` (`lib/reorderGrid.js`, unit-tested) works the new layout out the way the CSS grid will (fill the row from the left, wrap a section that does not fit, a row as tall as its tallest) and slides every shelf there at its own width, so the preview is the result. `sectionCollision` says which shelf the pointer is over: the one under it, else the nearest by its middle. One section per row (Grocery's stores, a phone) uses `verticalListSortingStrategy`, which is what Grocery has always used, with the nearest-middle rule in its own `collisionDetection`.
- **Two drag setups.** Grocery has its own `DndContext` (`useReorderSensors`: mouse 6px, touch 250 ms hold with 8px of give). Inventory's shelves sit in the app's one setup (`App.jsx`), because the items inside them drag between shelves there. Its one pointer sensor is `GripAwarePointerSensor`: everything else is picked up after the 200 ms hold, a press that starts on a grip (`data-reorder-grip`) the grip's way. (Two pointer sensors cannot share a setup: the second replaces the first.) `App.jsx`'s collision rule sends a `type: "shelf"` drag to `sectionCollision`, `handleDragEnd` leaves it to Inventory, and the floating-copy layer (`DragOverlay`) is left out while a shelf is carried, because with it up dnd-kit does not move the real shelf.
- **A shelf is also where an item is dropped.** It is one `useSortable` for both (`inv-shelf-<id>`); the dashed "drop here" look only shows for an item being carried (`inventoryItemId`), and the shelves do not slide for one.

## Recently removed

Anything that leaves Inventory waits in Recently removed (« Récemment retirés ») for 7 Quebec days, then goes for good. The Inventory row itself is deleted as it always was, and a copy waits in its own table (`PantryRemovedItem`, migration `20261013120000_pantry_removed_items`, add-only, deleted with its account), so `PantryInventoryItem` still means "in stock" and **nothing that reads Inventory can count a removed item**: not the matcher, I have it, Makeable, Use it up, the counts or the summary line.

- **What goes there, and how it's labelled** (`server/src/lib/removedItems.js`, `recordRemoved`, written in the same transaction as the delete): Used up and Tossed (the bulk bar and the pop-up, `POST /consume`, with the log line's id), Deleted (the pop-up's Remove, `DELETE /:id`), Used up for the recipe card's "I'm out of this" (`DELETE /:id?how=consumed`) and for a planned leftover whose day passed (`leftovers/settle`), and Cooked for "I cooked this" taking an item to zero (`POST /take-out`). Taking back an add (the add form's Undo, the finished view's Undo of its leftovers: `DELETE /:id?forget=1`) is not kept. A partial take-out never leaves Inventory.
- **The list:** `GET /api/pantry-inventory/removed` deletes entries older than 7 Quebec days, then lists the rest, newest first. The page reads it when it opens and again whenever a removal, an Undo or a Put back has landed (`removedChanged` in `lib/removedItems.js`, called by those calls in `api.js`; `hooks/useRemovedItems.js`). It is the shared `RemovedStrip`, collapsed under the shelves, shown only when something is in it: each row is the name struck through, "USED UP · YESTERDAY" (`removedHowLabel`, `removedWhenLabel`, Quebec days) and « Remettre » / Put back.
- **Put back** (`POST /removed/:id/put-back`, `handlePutBackRemoved` in `App.jsx`) restores the row as it was (`restoreRow`, the same code as Undo's `put-back`: same id, shelf, amount, use-by date, photo, leftover link; it keeps both where it was kept and which shelf it was on; a shelf that no longer exists puts it back on the default shelf of where it was kept), drops its "used up" / "tossed" log line, and takes it off the list. Like Undo it doesn't open the confirmation sheet (`CLAUDE.md`). The existing Undo toasts (`put-back`) also take the rows they restore off the list.

## Leftovers

One leftovers system. A **LEFTOVER item** is an Inventory item with `isLeftover` (and `recipeId`, the recipe it came from, none when typed in by hand), counted in portions (unit `portion`), kept in the fridge or the freezer (`location`), on Inventory's **Leftovers** shelf (a built-in shelf, `onShelf`; it is a shelf to look at, so `shelfOptions` leaves it out). `lib/leftovers.js` has the one set of keep times (the recipe's `fridgeLifeDays` or 4 in the fridge, 75 in the freezer) used by the finished view, the Planner's warning, thawing (`thawPatch`) and the item form's Leftovers mode. **The use-by is a Quebec calendar day** (see Quebec day): `leftoverExpiry` gives the day, the days left on the card count Quebec days, and `leftoverIsStale` compares the meal's day with the use-by day.

- **Inventory:** a leftover shows the recipe's photo, the LEFTOVER tag, Fridge or Freezer and the days left (in Compact under its name; in Tiles the tag sits on the photo); a frozen one's pop-up has "Move to fridge to thaw" (Undo). They don't drag between shelves. "+" on the Leftovers shelf (or the Leftovers chip in the add form) adds leftovers by hand: portions, Fridge or Freezer, and an optional recipe.
- **Planner:** the search's leftover cards (see Planner finder card) plan a leftover meal with `leftoverItemId` (a recipe meal, or a note « Restes · pizza » for leftovers with no recipe); the yellow border and tag as before, nothing on the grocery list. Leftover meals planned ahead (round button, Option-drag, Place leftovers) are linked when the recipe's leftovers are added. The "past fridge life" sticker uses `leftoverIsStale`: after the leftovers' use-by date when linked, else more days after the meal than the recipe keeps.
- **A day passes:** when the app opens, `POST /pantry-inventory/leftovers/settle` takes one portion off for each linked leftover meal before today, once (the meal gets `cookedAt`); leftovers at zero leave. The toast says so; Undo puts the portions back.
- **Home:** Use it up lists meals (leftovers and prepared meals) with 3 days or less first (`toUseItems`), with their tag; Cook with these searches the other items.

## Shelf and storage

Two things that used to share one field (`location`) are now two: **where an item is kept** (`PantryInventoryItem.location`: `fridge`, `freezer` or `pantry`; the USDA use-by estimate and shelf life read only this) and **which shelf it shows on** (`PantryInventoryItem.shelf`, an add-only nullable column, migration `20261020180000_inventory_shelf`: a custom section's id, or `fridge` / `freezer` / `pantry` / `prepared`; null is the default shelf). The default shelf is the one of where it is kept, **Prepared meals** for a prepared meal, **Leftovers** for leftovers.

- **One rule:** `shelfOf(item, shelfIds)` in `lib/shelves.js` (the server twin is `server/src/lib/shelves.js`). `onShelf`, `placeOnShelf` (what a drop writes), `keptThere` (what a change of storage writes), `storageLabelOf` and `defaultShelfOf` sit beside it. `shelfIds` is the shelves as the page shows them (`shelfSetOf(sections)`, a `Set` of the shelves that are showing that also knows where each deleted shelf's items go); a plain `Set` means no shelf is deleted. `server/src/shelfRule.test.js` fails if code compares `item.location` with a shelf, or reads `item.shelf`, anywhere else.
- **Prepared meals** is a built-in shelf like Leftovers (derived, with the shelf layout in `InventorySectionLayout` under `sectionId` `prepared`). A prepared meal is on it by default, and moving it to another shelf sticks. The shelf can be renamed, moved, resized and deleted like any other. If the old section of the same name is still there, empty, it is tagged "Old copy, empty" (see Inventory page, Edit mode).
- **The item pop-up** has **Stored in** (three cards: Fridge, Freezer, Pantry) and, separately, **Shelf** (a select: the three, Prepared meals and the person's own). Changing Stored in changes the use-by suggestion and never moves the item off a shelf that was chosen; the line under the Shelf select says whether it follows where it is kept, is a prepared meal, or was chosen.
- **Names:** a new or renamed shelf cannot take the name of another (409 `sectionExists`). A duplicate that already exists is left alone. A deleted shelf keeps its name, so a new shelf can't take it (the Add a shelf sheet says to bring it back instead).
- **Deleting a shelf (any shelf, built-in or Matt's own) HIDES it; nothing is deleted from the database and no item is written** (migration `20261021120000_inventory_shelf_hidden`, add-only: `InventorySectionLayout.hidden`, default false, and `movedTo`, nullable). The shelf's layout row stays, with its name, place and size. `shelfOf` follows `movedTo` (and on, if that shelf is hidden too; a missing target or a loop is the first shelf that is left), so an item whose shelf is hidden shows on the shelf it moved to, still saying where it is kept (« Congél. » on Fridge), and a **new** item that would have gone to the deleted shelf follows by itself, because it only says where it is kept. Bring the shelf back and every item is on it again by its own rule. Prepared meals and Leftovers fall back the same way. A drop on the shelf an item shows on only because of this changes nothing, and never where it is kept.
  - **The sheet** (`DeleteShelfSheet`, `components/InventoryShelfSheets.jsx`, in the shared Popup): "Delete « Freezer »?", "Its 4 items, and anything new that would go there, move to:" and a shelf picker of the shelves that are showing (never itself, never Leftovers), starting on `defaultMoveTarget` (`lib/shelfDelete.js`: Freezer to Fridge, Fridge to Pantry, Pantry to Fridge, Leftovers to Fridge, Prepared meals to Freezer, a shelf of his own to Pantry; the first shelf that is left if that one is gone), and a line that where things are kept and their use-by dates don't change. It saves one layout row (`hidden`, `movedTo`), and the toast ("Shelf « Freezer » deleted. Its items, and new ones, show on « Fridge ».") has Undo, which flips the row back exactly as it was.
  - **Bring back:** with a shelf deleted, "+ Add section" opens the Add a shelf sheet (`AddShelfSheet`): the name field, and under "Bring back a deleted shelf" a row for each deleted shelf with how many items return to it and a Bring back button (toast "Shelf « Freezer » is back." with Undo). It returns where it was in the order, with its name and size.
  - **The last shelf:** at least one shelf that can hold ingredients must be left (Leftovers does not count: it only holds leftovers). On the last one the sheet has no picker, says "This is your last shelf. Items need a shelf to sit on, so add another shelf first." and has one Got it button. The server refuses too (409 `lastShelf`), and refuses items going to a shelf that is hidden, missing, Leftovers or the shelf itself (400 `shelfMoveInvalid`).

  - **Stored in never comes from the shelf list:** the item form's cards are a fixed Fridge / Freezer / Pantry in the page's order (a deleted Freezer is still a place to keep things); its Shelf choice lists only the shelves that are showing, and says "The « Freezer » shelf is deleted, so this shows on « Fridge »."   - **The one real delete left** is the one-time sheet's « Supprimer » of the empty old « Prepared meals » copy (`DELETE /api/pantry-locations/:id`), as before.
- **Migration (nothing is deleted):** the column is added; every item's old `location` is copied into `shelf` when it was a section's id (its `location` is left, so where it is kept is unknown until Matt says); Matt's own « Prepared meals » shelf is merged into the built-in one (its items get `shelf = 'prepared'`, its layout row becomes the `prepared` row with his name, place and size); a tagged prepared meal with no shelf of its own is on Prepared meals by default (the rule, no row changes). ## Meal types and prepared recipes

A recipe can be **any number of meal types** at once (Chow Mein is a supper and a lunch) and can be **« Plat préparé »** (croissants: a breakfast AND nothing to cook), two separate things. Two add-only columns on `Recipe` (migration `20261021090000_recipe_meal_types`): `mealSlots String[]` (backfilled from the old single `mealSlot`, which stays, unused for reading, and is written as the first type so nothing that read it breaks) and `isPrepared Boolean` (off for every recipe until someone tags one). Nothing is dropped or renamed.

- **One reader:** `recipeSlots(recipe)` (list first, else the one old `mealSlot`, else an old meal tag; `[]` = not sorted yet). Filters use `hasSlot` (a recipe is under each of its types and counts once in totals); a card says the first type and `+1` for each other (`recipeSlotCaption`: « Dîner +1 »); the meal type is only that caption, never a colour.
- **Server:** `readMealTypes` / `readPrepared` (`server/src/lib/mealTypes.js`) read `mealSlots` (or the old `mealSlot`) and `isPrepared` for `POST` / `PUT /api/recipes`. An empty list is refused (400 `mealTypeRequired`): **a recipe is never saved without a type**; a recipe that was never given one (an import, an old one) simply has none until sorted.
- **The editor** (`RecipeEditor.jsx`, also the new-recipe pop-up): toggle chips for the types (`#re-slots`, role checkbox), the one shared `PreparedMealSwitch` (`components/PreparedMealSwitch.jsx`, also the Inventory form's), a « Masquer de Faisable » switch (`hiddenFromMakeable`, the shared `Switch`, saved with the form), and a suggestion line (« Ça ressemble à : Souper » + a button) when no type is on. Save with no type says so and scrolls to the chips.
- **Suggestions are rules, no AI** (`suggestMealSlots`, `suggestPrepared` in `server/src/lib/mealTypes.js`, `POST /api/recipes/suggest-types`, stateless): title and ingredient words in English and French (pancake, crêpe, cake, hummus, pickled, sauce, soup, mashed potatoes...; a ready-meal name, a bakery word, or a single ingredient suggests prepared). When the rules cannot decide the list is empty and the person picks. **Nothing is typed or tagged until the person confirms.**
- **The sort sheet** (`RecipeSortSheet.jsx`, the same sheet shell as Inventory's storage list): a banner on Recipes ("4 recipes have no meal type yet" + « Sort them ») and **every import** open it. Each recipe has the type chips and a Prepared meal chip, pre-picked from the suggestion and marked « guess » until touched; Confirm saves the ones with a type (`PUT /recipes/:id`), Not now changes nothing, a recipe left without a type stays.
- **The tag** (`PreparedTag`, see Prepared meals) goes on a prepared recipe's card through `RecipePhotoCard`'s `prepared` prop: top left of the Recipes card and the Makeable card (8px, 6px on a phone; the caption's left half gives way), in the bottom band above the title on a Cookbook tile, in the finder card's corner in place of the meal and time caps, S across the photo's edge on a Planner card, L upright first in the recipe pop-out's facts row.
- **Matching** (the one matcher, `lib/inventoryMatch.js`): a prepared recipe is had by **a prepared Inventory meal with the same name, by the matcher's rules, and by nothing else** (never an ingredient or a leftover), and its ingredients are not looked for among ingredients. `{ prepared: true }` on `matchingItems` / `haveItemFor` / `ingredientHave` / `shortBy` / `expiringSoon` is that (`poolOf`); `recipeLines`, `buildTakeOut` and `expiringItemsIn` read the recipe's own flag, so every page agrees. A prepared recipe "uses" no ingredient (`recipeUsesItem` is false: Cook with and "uses expiring" skip it), and a recipe that is not prepared never matches a prepared meal. `server/src/oneMealRule.test.js` guards the pool.
- **Grocery:** a prepared recipe's ingredient still goes on the list when it is not had. A row only prepared recipes need carries `fromPrepared` (`buildGroceryList`), and `splitAtHome` reads Already at home with the prepared pool for it; a row a real recipe needs too is an ingredient row.
- **"I have it"** on a prepared recipe's ingredient adds a **prepared meal** through the confirmation sheet (the draft carries `isPreparedMeal`; the row shows the tag). It is still the one sheet (`CLAUDE.md`).
- **A day passes** (`App.jsx`, once when the app opens, with the leftovers step): `GET /pantry-inventory/prepared-recipes/due` lists the planned prepared recipes (`isPrepared`, not a leftover, not settled) whose day is before today; the browser's matcher says which prepared meal each is (`lib/preparedRecipes.js` `preparedMatches`); `POST /pantry-inventory/prepared-recipes/settle` takes one box off each (a meal counted by weight or volume comes off whole, like a planned prepared meal: `portionsDue`) and marks every due meal settled (`cookedAt`), matched or not, so a prepared meal added later is never eaten for a past day. The server only checks (the meal is this person's, due; the item is their prepared meal). One message (« 1 repas retiré de votre inventaire : … ») with **Undo** (`put-back`; the meals stay settled).
- **Tests:** `lib/mealSlots.test.js`, `lib/finder.test.js`, `lib/preparedRecipe.test.js` (matcher, grocery, take-out, use soon), `lib/preparedRecipes.test.js`, `server/src/lib/mealTypes.test.js`, `server/src/routes/recipeMealTypes.db.test.js` (routes and the migration on sample rows), `server/src/routes/preparedRecipesDue.db.test.js`, `e2e/meal-types.spec.js` (Chow Mein under Lunch and Supper, Croissants hidden until the toggle, the sort sheet, an import, the day-passes step with Undo, French 390px for the chips, the switch, the sheet and the 104px Planner card). Screenshots: `docs/screenshots/recipes-meal-types/`.

## Prepared meals

A **prepared meal** is a ready-made dish in Inventory (a frozen lasagna, perogies, Matt's "Chicken, Broccoli and Cheddar Puff Pastry"). It is a meal, not an ingredient: `PantryInventoryItem.isPreparedMeal` (add-only column, migration `20261019120000_pantry_prepared_meal`, default false, so nothing changes until someone tags an item; it goes with its account). It is never set together with `isLeftover`, and Undo and Put back keep it (`removedItems.js` snapshots it, `restoreRow` writes it).

- **Tagging:** the **Prepared meal** switch (« Plat préparé ») in the item pop-up and in Add item (not in Leftovers mode). In Add item a **new** item whose name looks like a ready meal starts with the switch on and the line « Ça ressemble à un plat préparé », until the person touches it; `GET /pantry-inventory/suggest` answers `readyMeal` from `looksLikeReadyMeal` in `server/src/lib/dealAisle.js`, which shares its dish words with the flyer "Deli & ready meals" rule (flyers still sort exactly as before) and has accent-safe boundaries and an "ingredient, not a meal" list (dough, sauce, noodles, crust, « pâte »...). An item already in Inventory is never tagged for you, and the confirmation sheet (Done shopping, receipts, I have it) has no switch: tag it afterwards in its pop-up.
- **Looks:** ONE shared tag (`PreparedTag` in `components/RisoPills.jsx`; Matt's Claude Design, option 1d, kept in `docs/design/prepared-tag/`, README included): an ink pill (`--riso-ink-fixed`) with cream text (`--riso-knob`), a lower-case Bricolage 800 word, a takeout-box glyph and a `--riso-ink` outline, the opposite tilt of « restes » (+3°). Sizes: **S** (15px) on the Inventory tile, the compact row, Planner cards and the pop-up's photo; **M** (18px) on recipe cards and the Planner search card; **L** (22px) as the pop-up switch's label. A `sticker` is tilted and not clickable (on a photo or across a card's edge); in a line of text or chips it is upright. Where it sits is the CSS class of the place that draws it (`.inv-tile-prepared`: bottom left of the tile's white well; `.riso-planner-card-prepared`; `.rpc-tag` on a finder card; `.riso-itemform-card-prepared`; `.riso-home-week-day-prepared`), never a copy of the tag. `MealTag` (`InventoryItems.jsx`) draws the yellow LEFTOVER pill or this one. Yellow is LEFTOVER's, blue is "you have it" and green is "on your list", so it uses none of them. Its text is `preparedMeal.tag` in both languages. A French « plat préparé » at size S is about 75px, an English « prepared meal » about 85px, so both fit a 104px phone Planner card (a longer translation would drop the glyph at S before shrinking the text).
- **Planning and eating:** the Planner search offers it with the leftovers (`plannableMeals`, soonest use-by first) and plans it through `handlePlaceLeftoverItem`. It has no recipe, so it is held as a note, « Plat préparé · Lasagne » (`planner.preparedNote`), flagged `isLeftover` and linked by `leftoverItemId`, exactly like leftovers typed in by hand; the board does not draw that note as a note but as a **photo card** like a recipe's (`PlannerLinkedCard`, decided by `linkedMeal` in `lib/linkedMeal.js`: prepared by the linked item, or by the note's name once the item is gone). See "The planned photo card" below. The legend and the calendar's day preview wear the shared tag too. There is no "I ate it" button: when the planned day has passed, `POST /pantry-inventory/leftovers/settle` takes one box off (a prepared meal counted by weight or volume, like 900 g, comes off whole), once, with the toast and Undo; at zero it goes to Recently removed. The settle only eats from an item that is a meal (`portionsDue`), and `POST` / `PUT /planner` refuse a `leftoverItemId` that is an ingredient.
- **The planned photo card** (design: `docs/design/prepared-tag/`, "Planificateur"): a planned prepared meal, and leftovers typed in by hand whose item has a photo (a leftover with no photo stays the note), is a photo card on the computer board (cell 156 × 150, photo 58%), the phone board (104 × 104, photo 50px, the amount under the name, « 4 PORTIONS », « 1 SAC »), the calendar's day preview and Home's dinners strip. A prepared meal has a **3px ink border** (a recipe's is 2px, a leftover's is 3px yellow), a **white well that shows the product whole** (`object-fit: contain`, 4px 10px on a phone, 8px 14px 12px on a computer), the tag **S across the photo's bottom edge** (`right:5px; top:calc(58% - 10px)` on a computer, `right:1px; top:33px` on a phone inside the card's border) and the recipe card's ×. Leftovers keep the yellow border, a photo shown full-bleed and the yellow « restes » sticker. No photo: the striped placeholder (`#E9E4D8` / `#DDD6C6`, 135°). **The photo is read live through the Inventory link** (`linkedMeal` for the browser, `plannedDays` for the calendar), so a changed photo shows at once; **`PlannerEntry.photoUrl`** (add-only column, migration `20261020120000_planner_entry_photo`) is a copy kept only as the fallback once the item has left Inventory (the last box was eaten), so the card keeps its picture and the note's name says what it was. The server fills the copy when an entry is linked to an item (`POST` / `PUT /planner`), copies a changed photo to every entry that eats the item (`PUT /pantry-inventory/:id`), the migration filled it for entries that existed, and an Undo that puts a planned note back sends the copy it had (`restoreEntry`) for an item that is gone. Clicking the card opens the note's card (Remove note), like the note it is. Tested in `lib/linkedMeal.test.js`, `server/src/routes/plannerPhoto.db.test.js` and `e2e/prepared-tag.spec.js` (the 104px card in French and English, no sideways scroll).
- **The pop-up:** instead of "N of your recipes use X" a meal says « Un plat, pas un ingrédient » and has **Plan it** (the same slot picker as the Planner search's leftover cards, `requestPlanLeftover`). The switch's label **is** the tag (L), so the person sees what the item will carry, and when it is on the preview's photo wears the S sticker across its corner.
- **Undo of a removed planned note** now brings back its leftover mark and its link (`restoreEntry`), which hand-typed leftovers needed too.

**Forgot password page.** `AuthGate.jsx` shows one calm note (`auth-hint`) after the person sends their email: "sent" (`auth.forgotSent`) only when the server says `sent: true`, otherwise "We can't send emails yet. Ask Matt for a reset link." (`auth.forgotUnavailable`). ## Feedback

Friends testing the app send what is wrong or what they would like without leaving it, and Matt reads it all in one place. Nothing here is a second version of anything: the form is drawn in the shared Popup, its kind choice is the shared chips, its buttons are `riso-btn`, its box is the recipe card's notes textarea (one shared rule), "are you sure?" is `ConfirmDialog`, "Thanks!" is the shared Toast, and the inbox is built from Admin's heading and cards.

- **Ways in** (all open the same `FeedbackForm`, which `App.jsx` keeps and offers through `FeedbackContext`, read with `useFeedback()`): the avatar menu's **Send feedback**; the last card of Help (`#help-feedback`, with the same button); a **Report a problem** link (`ReportProblemLink`) beside the load-error bar, the finished view's error line, the Inventory item form's error and the recipe form's save error; and the crash screen. Cook mode and Store mode cover the screen and have no header, so they have no entry of their own (their errors would still show the link). A toast has none: it goes in five seconds and the one toast only has Undo.
- **The form:** a title, a line saying where it goes, the kind (`FEEDBACK_KINDS`: `wrong` / `idea` / `other`, as chips that wrap, so « Quelque chose ne marche pas » never runs out of a phone), the message box (2,000 characters at most, a count from 1,800), a grey line listing what is attached, an error line, **Cancel** and **Send** (off until there are words). The kind starts on Other from the menu and Help, and on Something's wrong from an error. Closing with words typed asks first (`ConfirmDialog`: Keep writing / Discard); Escape closes only this form, taken in the capture phase, so the pop-up under it (the item form) stays. The form is drawn above every page and pop-up (z-index 350, below the question 400 and the toast 500) and in a portal, so it opens over Cook mode and the recipe card.
- **Sending:** `POST /api/feedback`. A failure keeps the text and says "Couldn't send it. Your text is still here: try again."; the server's own words (in the reader's language) show for "too long" (400) and "you've sent a lot" (429). Success closes the form and shows the toast « Merci! » / "Thanks!".
- **What the app attaches, without asking, and nothing else** (`feedbackContext` in `lib/feedback.js`): the page (the tab's name: `home`, `planner`, `help`...; never a recipe's title), the language, phone or computer (the same `PHONE_QUERY` as `useIsPhone`) and the window size, and the app version (`lib/appVersion.js`: the short commit code that `vite.config.js` puts in at build time, Render's `RENDER_GIT_COMMIT`; `dev` locally). The browser is read by the server from the request's own User-Agent (`browserLabel`: "Safari 17 on iOS", never the full text), and the time is the server's. No screenshots, no recipe or Inventory content. From the crash screen or an error link the form also sends the error's **name** only (`errorNameOf`: "TypeError"; or a fixed label from `ERROR_LABELS` such as "LoadError"), never its text, which can hold anything.
- **Crash screen:** `CrashBoundary` in `main.jsx`, twice: around the signed-in app (Reload and Report a problem) and around everything, the login page included (Reload only: nobody is signed in to report). `lib/feedback.js` keeps the page App.jsx last set (`setCurrentPage`), because the crash screen draws when App has stopped.
- **Saved in** the `Feedback` table (migration `20261018120000_feedback`, add-only; a row goes with its user): the words, kind, page, language, device, screen, browser, app version, error name, `doneAt`, `archivedAt` (migration `20261019190000_feedback_archive`, add-only: null = in the inbox) and the time. The words and details are never edited; the only things that change are Matt's Done tick and his Archive.
- **Limits** (`server/src/lib/feedback.js`; the client keeps the same maximum, checked by `lib/feedback.test.js`): 1 to 2,000 characters after trimming, a known kind, details cut or set to "unknown" rather than refused. Per account, **5 in any 10 minutes and 30 in a Quebec day** (`feedbackLimitHit`, counted from the Feedback table itself, so a restart or the free server sleeping never resets it): over the limit the answer is 429 with a message in the reader's language. A burst of simultaneous sends can slip one or two past it.
- **The email to Matt:** when `FEEDBACK_EMAIL_TO` is set, `sendFeedbackEmail` (`server/src/lib/mailer.js`, through the one sender) sends a short email after the answer is given, so the tester never waits and a failed email never fails the feedback: who, the kind, the words, the details and a link to the inbox, in the language of the account that owns that address (else French). Every typed word is escaped and none is in the subject. It sends with `remember: false`, so it neither marks email as failing nor ready for the Forgot password page. Without `FEEDBACK_EMAIL_TO` no email is sent and the inbox still has everything.
- **The inbox:** every route is behind `requireAuth` and `requireAdmin` (401 / 403): `GET /api/admin/feedback?box=inbox|archive&before=<cursor>` (one page of 200, newest first, with the sender's email; `nextCursor` for the next, older page; `counts: { inbox, archive, notDone }` over everything), `PATCH /api/admin/feedback/:id { done?, archived? }` (Done tick, Archive and Back to inbox; answers `{ item, counts }`), `POST /api/admin/feedback/archive-done` (every done one in the inbox, answers the `ids` it moved) and `POST /api/admin/feedback/unarchive { ids }` (the Undo of that). A row shows a kind pill, who sent it, Today / Yesterday / the date and the time, the words (line breaks kept), the details (page, language, device and size, browser, app version, error), a **Done** button that ticks and un-ticks, and **Archive** (in the Archive tab: **Back to inbox**). **Done and Archive are separate:** Done = handled, Archive = out of sight; archiving doesn't tick Done, a done one stays done in the Archive, and **nothing is ever deleted by archiving**. The pill next to the title counts the not-done ones **in the inbox only**; each tab has its own `CountPill` (how many are in it). Archive, Back to inbox and "Archive all done" show the shared Toast (`onToast`) with Undo; the move is made at once, and Undo moves it back (for "Archive all done", exactly the ids it moved, not what was archived before) and reads the tab again. **Paging:** each tab shows 200 (`FEEDBACK_LIST_LIMIT`), then "Show older" asks for the next 200 with the cursor (the last row's sent time and id, so rows with the same time are neither repeated nor skipped); the counts and "Archive all done" are always over everything, not the loaded page. Admin pages with their own address are `/admin` and `/admin/feedback` (`adminTabFor`, `pathForTab` in `lib/admin.js`); a non-admin on it sees « Accès refusé. » and the menu entry is not shown.
- **Help** says how to send feedback, what is attached and that Report a problem exists (`help.sections.account` in both languages, the `[[feedbackButton]]` copy in `lib/helpCopies.js`), and ends with the card above.
- **Tests:** `server/src/lib/feedback.test.js`, `feedback.db.test.js`, `mailer.test.js`; `client/src/lib/feedback.test.js`, `admin.test.js`; `e2e/feedback.spec.js` (the whole file runs one test after the other, because the inbox tests read the whole table's counts and "Archive all done" moves every done one; no other spec file adds feedback. The French phone check of the tabs and buttons is in it too) and the French checks in `e2e/french.spec.js`. Screenshots: `docs/screenshots/feedback/`.

## Where things live in `App.jsx`

`App.jsx` owns the data and the shared pieces above: `openRecipeCard`, `requestPlan`, `showToast`. A page gets them as props. A new page that needs a recipe opened, a recipe planned or a message shown uses these and does not make its own. It also gives `.app` the class `is-home` while the Home tab shows (see Home's two screens); no other page has a shell of its own.
